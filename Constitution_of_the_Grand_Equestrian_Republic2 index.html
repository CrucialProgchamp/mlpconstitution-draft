We, the People of the Grand Equestrian Republic, acknowledging the enduring wisdom of Aristotle that true democracy is not the rule of elected elites, but the governance of common citizens chosen by the hand of fate — do ordain and establish this Constitution.

We hold these truths to be self-evident: that all citizens are equal in their capacity to govern; that the accumulation of political power in the hands of the wealthy and persuasive is the beginning of oligarchy; and that a government truly of the people must, by structure and not by aspiration, include the people in the fullness of their diversity.

Guided by four pillars — Unity, Responsibility, Wisdom, and the Common Good — this Republic shall be governed not by those who campaign the hardest, but by those who serve with integrity when chosen by lot.

Article I
⚖
Fundamental Principles & Rights
Part A — Foundational Philosophy
§ 1.
The Nature of Democracy
This Republic affirms Aristotle's principle that democracy is characterized by the rule of the free-born many, by equality among citizens, and by the rotation of civic authority among all eligible persons. No permanent political class shall be permitted to emerge. Public office is a civic duty, not a career.
§ 2.
The Four Pillars
The governance of this Republic shall be guided at all times by four foundational values: (a) Unity — the indivisibility of the citizenry irrespective of wealth, class, or status; (b) Responsibility — the obligation of every citizen and official to act in service of the common good; (c) Wisdom — the deliberate cultivation of informed, balanced, and evidence-based governance; and (d) the Common Good — the principle that all policy shall prioritize the flourishing of the whole community over any faction or individual.
§ 3.
Rejection of All Non-Democratic Rule
The Grand Equestrian Republic declares, without reservation or exception, that the following systems of governance are fundamentally incompatible with the dignity, freedom, and equality of its citizens, and are expressly and permanently prohibited in all their forms, whether adopted openly, gradually, or under the guise of necessity:

(a) Oligarchy — the concentration of political power in the hands of a privileged few, whether defined by wealth, lineage, profession, or social connection. Any law, institution, or practice that systematically advantages any group in access to public office on grounds other than the equal lot of citizenship shall be void;

(b) Tyranny — the exercise of unlimited, unchecked, or unaccountable power by any single person, body, or institution over the life, liberty, or civic participation of citizens. No person and no institution shall hold power that is not bounded by this Constitution, subject to oversight, and revocable by constitutional process;

(c) Theocracy — the governance of the Republic according to religious doctrine, clerical authority, or divine mandate. No religious institution, scripture, or ecclesiastical body may direct, override, or substitute for the constitutional processes of this Republic. Faith is a matter of personal conscience; it is not a source of civic law;

(d) Plutocracy — the rule of the wealthy by virtue of their wealth. The possession of material resources shall confer no civic advantage — no greater voice, no greater eligibility, and no greater access to justice than that enjoyed by the least prosperous citizen. The Republic shall actively counteract the conversion of economic power into political power;

(e) Ochlocracy — the tyranny of the mob; the rule of passion, fear, and prejudice unconstrained by law, deliberation, or the rights of minorities. Democracy is not the unchecked will of a momentary majority — it is the reasoned, lawful, and rights-respecting governance of all citizens. No majority, however large, may use the instruments of this Republic to strip any person of the rights guaranteed by this Constitution;

(f) Technocracy — the substitution of expert rule for citizen governance. Expertise is indispensable to good deliberation; it is not a substitute for democratic decision-making. Technical advisors shall inform the Assembly and the Council — they shall never replace them. The final authority on all questions of governance rests with citizens chosen by lot, not with those who claim superior knowledge;

(g) Authoritarianism — any system that suppresses political freedom, curtails civic participation, punishes dissent, controls information, or rules by fear rather than consent. The Republic is constituted in opposition to this principle in all its manifestations, whether overt dictatorship or the creeping curtailment of liberties under the banner of order, security, or efficiency;

(h) Foreign Domination — the direct or indirect control of this Republic's governance by any foreign government, organization, or individual not bound by democratic principles and the consent of the citizens of this Republic. The sovereignty of this Republic resides in its citizens alone and may not be alienated, surrendered, or compromised by any treaty, debt, or dependency;

(i) Dynasticism — the inheritance, transmission, or reservation of civic power within families, bloodlines, or hereditary classes. No office, position, privilege, or civic advantage of this Republic may be inherited or reserved by virtue of birth or familial connection;

(j) Demagoguery — the manipulation of citizens through fear, falsehood, hatred, and emotional incitement to acquire or consolidate power outside constitutional processes. The Republic recognizes demagoguery as among the most insidious threats to democracy and commits itself to civic education, free press, and institutional resilience as its primary defenses.

This section shall be interpreted broadly and purposively. Any system, practice, or conduct that resembles, approximates, or effectively produces any of the above forms of non-democratic rule — regardless of the name it bears or the justification it offers — is incompatible with this Constitution and shall be treated accordingly.
Part B — The Bill of Rights
§ 9.
Rights of Children
Every child within the Republic is entitled to protection from exploitation, abuse, and neglect; to education, nutrition, shelter, and healthcare; and to a voice in matters that affect their lives, proportionate to their age and maturity. The state shall act as guardian of last resort for any child without adequate parental protection. No child under the age of sixteen may be compelled to perform labour inconsistent with their dignity and development.
§ 10.
Rights of Minorities & Indigenous Peoples
Every linguistic, cultural, ethnic, and indigenous community within the Republic has the right to maintain, practice, and transmit its language, culture, and traditions. The Republic shall not assimilate or suppress any minority community by law or policy. Indigenous peoples shall have the right to participate in decisions affecting their ancestral lands and traditional practices. The Civic Registry shall ensure that minority communities are proportionally represented in all civic lotteries.
§ 4.
Political Equality
Every citizen is equal before this Constitution. No citizen's voice, vote, or eligibility for civic duty shall be weighted, diminished, or removed on the basis of wealth, property, gender, religion, ethnicity, or social class.
§ 5.
Civil Liberties
All citizens are guaranteed the following rights, which no act of government may abridge: freedom of conscience and religion; freedom of expression and peaceful assembly; the right to due process and a fair hearing before citizens chosen by lot; the right to privacy in one's person and home; and the right to education sufficient to participate meaningfully in civic life.
§ 6.
Freedom of the Press & Public Information
A free and independent press is indispensable to an informed citizenry and to the health of democratic governance. The Republic shall not own, control, license, or suppress any media outlet. Journalists shall not be compelled to reveal their sources. The Republic shall maintain a Public Broadcasting Service, governed by a citizen board selected by lot, providing factual civic information free from government direction. No public official may bring a defamation action against a journalist for reporting on matters of public interest undertaken in good faith.
§ 7.
Social Rights
The Republic recognizes the following social rights as necessary for genuine political equality: the right to adequate nutrition, shelter, and healthcare; the right to a meaningful livelihood; and the right to equal access to public institutions and civic information.
§ 8.
Civic Duty
Participation in civic life is both a right and a duty. Citizens selected by lot for public service are obligated to serve unless exempted by the Exemption Tribunal on grounds of genuine hardship, incapacity, or irreconcilable conflict of interest.
Part C — Limitations on Government Power
§ 11.
Enumerated Limitations
The government of the Grand Equestrian Republic — in all its branches, commissions, bodies, and offices — shall not, under any circumstance: (a) enact any law, decree, or order that abridges the rights enumerated in Part B of this Article; (b) detain any citizen without a warrant issued by a Citizen Jury; (c) compel any citizen to testify against themselves in any criminal proceeding; (d) subject any citizen to double jeopardy — that is, prosecution for the same offence following acquittal or conviction; (e) enact any law that applies retroactively to make criminal an act that was lawful when committed; (f) seize private property without due process, a lawful court order, and just compensation; (g) impose collective punishment upon a community, family, or group for the acts of an individual; (h) censor, suppress, or compel any form of speech, publication, or expression except as narrowly authorized by Fundamental Law to prevent imminent harm; (i) conduct surveillance of any citizen without a warrant issued by a Citizen Jury upon demonstrated probable cause; or (j) deny any person within its jurisdiction the equal protection of the law.
§ 12.
The Presumption of Liberty
In any dispute between a citizen and an organ of the state regarding the scope of a right or the extent of governmental power, the presumption shall always favor the citizen. The burden of justification rests upon the state to demonstrate, before a Citizen Jury, that its action is: authorized by a specific provision of this Constitution or a law passed pursuant to it; necessary to achieve a legitimate constitutional purpose; proportionate to the harm it seeks to prevent; and the least restrictive means available to achieve that purpose. An action of the state that fails any one of these four tests is unconstitutional.
§ 13.
Horizontal Application
The rights guaranteed in Part B of this Article bind not only the institutions of the state but also, where applicable, private persons, corporations, and organizations that exercise significant power over the civic, economic, or social life of citizens. No private entity that holds a position of practical dominance over another person's access to livelihood, housing, education, or public life may invoke its private status to justify the violation of that person's fundamental rights. The Assembly shall by Fundamental Law define the categories of private relationships to which horizontal application extends.
§ 14.
Non-Derogation Clause
Nothing in this Constitution shall be interpreted to deny, restrict, or derogate from any right or freedom that exists independently of this Constitution under international human rights law, customary law, or the common conscience of civilized peoples. Where any provision of this Constitution appears to conflict with a more protective standard of human rights existing outside it, the more protective standard shall prevail. The rights enumerated in this Constitution are a floor, not a ceiling.

Article II
🏛
Citizenship & Civic Eligibility
§ 1.
Citizenship
Citizenship is acquired by birth within the Republic, by birth to a citizen parent, or by a naturalization process of no fewer than five years of lawful residency, civic participation, and demonstrated commitment to the values of this Constitution.
§ 2.
Civic Eligibility
All citizens aged eighteen years and above are eligible for selection to civic office. However, in the tradition of Athenian democracy — which required citizens to be at least thirty years of age before serving in the Boule, the Heliaia, and senior magistracies — the following senior offices of the Grand Equestrian Republic shall require a minimum age of thirty years: membership of the Council (Boule); membership of any Citizen Jury in criminal matters or Constitutional Review matters; service as a Legal Guide; service as an Archon; membership of the Guardian Commission; membership of the Office of the Public Auditor; membership of the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate; and service as Crisis Coordinator under Article XXIV. Citizens aged eighteen to twenty-nine remain fully eligible for the Assembly (Ekklesia), Neighborhood and District Assemblies, Neighborhood Courts in civil matters, committee service, and all petition and referendum processes. This age distinction reflects the Aristotelian principle that sustained civic experience deepens the wisdom and impartiality required for the Republic's most consequential judgments. Eligibility is subject to the following disqualifications regardless of age: conviction of corruption or abuse of public trust within the preceding ten years; a finding of severe cognitive incapacity by a medical panel; or voluntary renunciation of citizenship.
§ 3.
Civic Registry
The Republic shall maintain a permanent, publicly verifiable Civic Registry of all eligible citizens. This Registry shall be the sole source from which all civic selections by lot are drawn. The integrity of the Registry is paramount and shall be overseen by an independent Guardian Commission.
The Civic Oath
§ 4.
Universal Requirement
Every citizen selected by lot or appointed to any civic duty — including but not limited to service in the Assembly, the Council, any Citizen Jury, the Guardian Commission, the Office of the Public Auditor, the Referendum Commission, the Boundaries Commission, any Ministerial office, any Legal Guide position, and any military commission — shall, before assuming their duties, publicly swear or solemnly affirm the Civic Oath as prescribed by this Article. No civic duty may be exercised, no vote cast, no order issued, and no judgment rendered by any person who has not first sworn or affirmed the Civic Oath in the presence of a witness designated by the Court Registry or the Guardian Commission. Service rendered without the Oath is void from the moment of its performance.
§ 5.
The Text of the Civic Oath
The Civic Oath shall be administered in the following form, spoken aloud or affirmed in writing by the citizen before witnesses: "I, [full name], citizen of the Grand Equestrian Republic, having been chosen to serve the people in the office of [position], do solemnly swear — or solemnly affirm — the following: I will uphold, protect, and defend the Constitution of the Grand Equestrian Republic as the supreme law of this Republic, above all other laws, loyalties, and allegiances. I will serve with honesty, impartiality, and dedication to the common good. I will place the welfare of all citizens above any personal, factional, ideological, or private interest. I will not allow my religious beliefs, political ideology, party affiliation, or personal philosophy to override my constitutional duties or to grant advantage or disadvantage to any person before me. I will not act in the service of any foreign power, non-democratic authority, secret society, or movement that does not recognize the equal dignity and civic rights of all citizens of this Republic. I accept this duty freely, I will discharge it faithfully, and I will surrender it honestly when my term of service concludes. To this I commit, under the full weight of my conscience and the law of this Republic."
§ 6.
Swearing and Affirming
No citizen may be compelled to swear the Civic Oath in a religious form. Any citizen who objects to swearing on grounds of conscience, religion, or personal belief shall instead solemnly affirm the Oath, which carries identical legal force. The Republic shall not prescribe, prefer, or discriminate between the sworn and affirmed forms. The only requirement is that the commitment be made publicly, deliberately, and with full understanding of its content and consequences.
§ 7.
Explicit Renunciations
The Civic Oath constitutes, by operation of this Constitution, an explicit renunciation for the duration of civic service of the following allegiances and influences: (a) Ideological capture — no civic officer may act primarily in the service of a political ideology, movement, or party platform rather than the Constitution and the common good; (b) Religious direction — no civic officer may allow religious doctrine, clerical instruction, or theological mandate to override their constitutional duties or to discriminate in the application of law; (c) Non-democratic loyalty — no civic officer may pledge, display, or act upon loyalty to any flag, symbol, authority, or movement that does not recognize the equal civic dignity of all persons; (d) Foreign authority — no civic officer may act under the direction, influence, or inducement of any foreign government, foreign organization, or foreign person; and (e) Factional interest — no civic officer may exercise their powers for the benefit of any private group, family, corporation, or association to the detriment of the public interest. These renunciations are not a restriction on personal belief — citizens remain free to hold any faith, philosophy, or political view privately. They are a restriction on the exercise of civic power.
§ 8.
Breach of the Civic Oath
Breach of the Civic Oath is among the most serious offences against the Republic. A breach occurs when a civic officer demonstrably acts in a manner irreconcilable with the commitments sworn, including: rendering decisions motivated by ideology, religion, or factional loyalty rather than law and evidence; acting under foreign direction or inducement; concealing a material conflict of interest; or displaying loyalty to a non-democratic authority while exercising civic power. Allegations of breach shall be heard by a Citizen Jury of thirty-one persons. Upon conviction, the penalty shall include: immediate removal from civic office; permanent disqualification from all future civic service; referral for criminal prosecution where applicable; and public recording of the breach in the Official Gazette of the Republic.
§ 9.
Oath Registry
The Guardian Commission shall maintain a permanent, publicly accessible Oath Registry recording the name, position, date, and form of oath of every citizen who has sworn or affirmed the Civic Oath. The Oath Registry shall be published in full on the Official Civic Record and updated within seventy-two hours of each new swearing. Citizens have an unqualified right to inspect the Oath Registry at any time. No civic act performed by a person not listed in the Oath Registry as having sworn the Oath for their current position shall have legal force.

The Assembly (Ekklesia) — Legislature
The legislative power of the Republic resides in the people — three thousand citizens, chosen by lot, equal in voice and in duty.
Structure and Composition
§ 1.
Establishment
The legislative power of the Republic is vested in the National Assembly, composed of three thousand citizens selected by lot from the Civic Registry.
§ 2.
Term of Service
Each member of the Assembly shall serve a term of two years. No citizen may serve more than six non-consecutive terms in the Assembly across their lifetime, ensuring broad civic participation while allowing experienced members to contribute over time.
§ 3.
Selection Process
Selection to the Assembly shall be conducted by the Guardian Commission through a transparent, publicly witnessed lottery. The process shall be stratified to ensure proportional representation of age, gender, region, and socioeconomic background, so that the Assembly mirrors the composition of the citizenry.
§ 4.
Compensation
Members of the Assembly shall receive compensation sufficient to enable any citizen — regardless of income — to serve without financial hardship. No additional income from outside sources shall be permitted during service.
Powers of the Assembly
§ 5.
Legislative Authority
The Assembly shall hold the sole power to enact, amend, and repeal laws of the Republic. No law shall take effect without the approval of an absolute majority of the Assembly.
§ 6.
Budgetary Power
The Assembly controls the national budget. No public funds may be appropriated, expended, or committed without explicit Assembly authorization.
§ 7.
Treaty Ratification
All international treaties and binding agreements must be ratified by a two-thirds majority of the Assembly before they take legal effect within the Republic.
§ 8.
Deliberation Standards
Before voting on any legislation, the Assembly shall receive briefings from relevant subject-matter experts, consider evidence from affected communities, and engage in structured deliberation. The Assembly shall establish rules of procedure ensuring that all members have equal opportunity to speak.
Legislative Procedure
§ 9.
Right of Proposal
Any member of the Assembly may introduce a bill for consideration. Additionally, any citizen or group of citizens may propose legislation directly to the Assembly by submitting a petition bearing the verified signatures of no fewer than one percent of the total registered citizenry. Upon verification by the Guardian Commission, citizen-initiated bills shall be placed before the Assembly with equal standing to member-introduced bills and must be debated within sixty days of submission.
§ 10.
Classification of Laws
All proposed legislation shall be classified upon introduction into one of two categories: (a) Ordinary Laws — governing routine matters of public administration, taxation, civil regulation, and social services; and (b) Fundamental Laws — governing matters of national security, constitutional rights, the structure of government, the judiciary, international treaties, and any measure imposing penalties of the gravest severity. The classification shall be determined by the Guardian Commission. In cases of dispute, the matter shall be resolved by a Constitutional Jury.
§ 11.
Deliberation Period
No bill may be put to a vote without a mandatory deliberation period: fourteen days for Ordinary Laws and forty-five days for Fundamental Laws. During this period, the Assembly shall hold open hearings, receive testimony from subject-matter experts and affected communities, and publish all proposed text in full for public review. No deliberation period may be waived except in a declared national emergency, and only with the unanimous consent of the Council.
§ 12.
Voting Thresholds
The threshold for passage shall be determined by the classification of the law: (a) Ordinary Laws require approval by a simple majority — that is, more than half of Assembly members present and voting, provided that no fewer than one thousand five hundred members constitute a quorum; (b) Fundamental Laws require approval by a two-thirds supermajority of all seated Assembly members, regardless of attendance. In all votes, members must be physically or verifiably present. Proxy voting is prohibited.
§ 13.
Public Record & Promulgation
Every vote cast in the Assembly shall be individually recorded and publicly attributed to each member by name. No anonymous voting shall be permitted. Upon passage, every law shall be published in full in the Official Gazette of the Republic within seventy-two hours and shall not take effect until thirty days after publication, allowing citizens time for review and, where applicable, petition for Constitutional Review.
§ 14.
Citizen Veto
Within the thirty-day promulgation window, a passed law may be suspended pending a popular referendum if a petition bearing the signatures of three percent of the registered citizenry is submitted to the Guardian Commission. The referendum shall be held within ninety days. The law shall be repealed if a majority of participating citizens vote against it, provided turnout reaches at least forty percent of the registered citizenry.

The Council (Boule) — Executive
This Republic shall have no President, no Prime Minister, no singular sovereign. The executive power belongs to five hundred citizens, governing as equals.
Structure
§ 1.
Establishment
Executive power is vested in the National Council, composed of five hundred citizens selected by lot from the Civic Registry. The Council has no single head. It acts collectively and by consensus wherever possible, and by majority vote where consensus cannot be reached.
§ 2.
Term of Service
Members of the Council serve one-year terms on a rotating basis, with two hundred and fifty seats renewed every six months to ensure continuity of governance. No citizen may serve more than four terms on the Council.
§ 3.
No Head of State
This Republic shall have no President, Prime Minister, Chancellor, or equivalent singular executive authority. The Republic is governed collectively. Any ceremonial representation of the Republic shall be performed by a rotating Spokesperson elected from among Council members for a period of ninety days.
The Prytany & Epistates — Rotational Executive
§ 3-A.
The Prytany
In the tradition of the Athenian Prytany, the Council shall at all times maintain a standing Prytany — a rotating sub-committee of fifty Council members selected by internal lot, serving a term of thirty-five days, responsible for the continuous daily administration of the Republic between full Council sessions. The Prytany is convened at all times, including when the full Council is not in session. It prepares the agenda of full Council sessions; receives diplomatic communications, ambassadors, and messengers on behalf of the Republic; manages the Republic's official seals and instruments of state; and ensures that no administrative function of the Republic is interrupted between Council sessions. The ten tribes or regional groupings of the Council shall each provide the Prytany in rotation, ensuring that every regional community has equal opportunity to serve in the standing executive capacity.
§ 3-B.
The Epistates — Daily Chair
Each day that the Prytany is in session, one member shall serve as Epistates — the Daily Chair of the Republic — selected by lot from among the Prytany members who have not yet served as Epistates in the current Prytany term. The Epistates shall: preside over all sessions of the Prytany and any full Council session occurring on that day; hold the official seal of the Grand Equestrian Republic for that day; hold the keys to the public treasury and the constitutional records for that day; receive any ambassador, diplomatic communication, or formal petition presented to the Republic on that day; and sign any instrument of state requiring executive certification on that day. No citizen may serve as Epistates more than once during the same Prytany term. This daily rotation ensures that the executive function of the Republic rests in the hands of a different citizen each day — the most complete expression of the Aristotelian ideal that all citizens take turns in governing.
Powers of the Council
§ 4.
Executive Administration
The Council shall administer the laws of the Republic, oversee all ministries and public agencies, and implement the budget as authorized by the Assembly.
§ 4-A.
Supervision & Evaluation of All Magistrates
In the tradition of the Athenian Boule, the Council shall supervise, evaluate, and direct the work of every civic magistrate, Department Director-General, Agency head, and civil officer of the Grand Equestrian Republic. This supervisory power includes: receiving regular reports from all civic officers on the conduct of their duties; assessing whether each officer is fulfilling the mandate of their office faithfully and constitutionally; referring evidence of misconduct, dereliction, or constitutional violation to the Public Auditor for Dokimasia proceedings and to the Courts where criminal conduct is suspected; and issuing formal directions to civic officers regarding the execution of their constitutional duties, within the bounds of their respective mandates. The Council's supervisory role does not extend to the Courts, the Guardian Commission, the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate, or the Judicial Rules Commission, whose independence is constitutionally guaranteed. The Council shall publish an annual Magistracy Performance Report assessing the conduct of all civic offices under its supervision.
§ 5.
Emergency Powers
In times of urgent national crisis, the Council may issue emergency directives effective for no more than thirty days without Assembly ratification. All emergency directives must be presented to the Assembly within seventy-two hours and must receive majority approval to remain in force.
§ 6.
Ministerial Oversight
The Council shall appoint and supervise Ministers of State, who shall be selected from a pool of qualified professionals recommended by independent expert panels. Ministers are administrators, not politicians, and shall serve fixed administrative terms of three years.
§ 7.
Accountability
The Council is accountable to the Assembly. The Assembly may, by two-thirds vote, dissolve the Council and trigger a new selection if the Council has demonstrably violated its mandate, acted corruptly, or failed to execute the law.
Foreign Policy & Defense
§ 8.
Foreign Affairs
The Council shall conduct the foreign affairs of the Republic in accordance with laws and treaties ratified by the Assembly. It shall maintain diplomatic relations, negotiate international agreements, and represent the Republic before foreign nations and international bodies. No binding foreign commitment may be made without ultimate Assembly ratification.
§ 9.
Defense & Civil Security
The Council shall oversee the defense forces of the Republic, which exist solely to protect the citizenry and territory from external threat. The defense forces shall at all times remain subordinate to civilian authority as exercised by the Council and Assembly. No offensive military action may be initiated without the approval of a two-thirds supermajority of the full Assembly. The Council may order defensive measures immediately in response to active attack, but must seek Assembly ratification within seventy-two hours.
§ 10.
Declaration of War
A formal declaration of war is among the gravest acts a Republic may undertake. It shall require: approval by a two-thirds supermajority of the full Assembly; a concurrent advisory referendum put to the citizenry within thirty days; and a finding by a Constitutional Jury that the declaration is consistent with this Constitution. No declaration of war may suspend or diminish the fundamental rights guaranteed in Article II.
Committee System
§ 11.
Standing Committees of the Council
The Council shall organize itself into ten Standing Committees, each responsible for a defined domain of executive administration: (a) Finance & Budget Administration; (b) Justice & Civil Rights; (c) Public Health & Social Welfare; (d) Education & Culture; (e) Infrastructure & Environment; (f) Defense & Public Safety; (g) Foreign Affairs & Treaties; (h) Agriculture & Commerce; (i) Science & Technology; and (j) Civic & Constitutional Oversight. Each Committee shall be composed of fifty members selected by internal lot from the Council, renewed every six months. Committees shall coordinate the Council's administrative oversight of the relevant Department and shall report to the full Council monthly.
§ 12.
Committee Powers
Each Standing Committee shall: conduct in-depth review of the administration of its domain; summon Directors-General, Department officers, and Agency heads to give account of their administration; propose Council directives within its domain for consideration by the full Council; and publish a monthly Committee Report on the state of administration within its jurisdiction. No major administrative directive within a Committee's domain shall be issued by the Council without prior Committee review, except in declared emergencies. Committee reports shall be published in the Official Gazette within seven days of adoption.
§ 13.
Committee Integrity
Committee membership shall rotate every six months by internal lot to prevent the entrenchment of influence within any committee. No member may serve on the same committee for two consecutive rotation periods. All committee hearings shall be open to the public and fully recorded. Committee Chairs shall be selected by lot from among committee members and shall serve for three months only. A Committee Chair may not serve as Chair of any committee for two consecutive periods.

The Tribunal Court (Dikasteria) — Judiciary
Justice is not the province of a robed elite. In this Republic, the community — fully informed and properly guided — is the most legitimate arbiter of justice.
Standing & Jurisdiction
§ 1.
Standing — Who May Bring a Case
The following persons and bodies shall have standing to bring proceedings before the Courts of the Grand Equestrian Republic: (a) Any citizen who has suffered, or is at imminent risk of suffering, a direct and personal harm as a result of the act, omission, law, or decision being challenged; (b) Any group of citizens acting collectively where the harm affects them as a class and individual proceedings would be impractical; (c) Any civic institution — including the Guardian Commission, the Public Auditor, the Referendum Commission, and the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate — acting within the scope of their constitutional mandate; (d) The Assembly, by resolution of simple majority, to challenge an executive act it believes unconstitutional; (e) The Council, by majority vote, to challenge a law it believes was enacted in violation of constitutional procedure; and (f) Any resident non-citizen whose fundamental rights under Article I have been violated by an act of the state within the Republic's jurisdiction. Standing shall be determined by the Court Registry upon filing and may be challenged by any party within fourteen days.
§ 2.
Jurisdiction of Neighborhood Courts
Neighborhood Courts shall have exclusive first-instance jurisdiction over: minor civil disputes between residents of the same Neighborhood where the amount in dispute does not exceed a threshold set by Fundamental Law; neighborhood infractions and by-law violations; disputes concerning local civic matters; and minor interpersonal disputes where all parties consent. Neighborhood Courts shall have no jurisdiction over criminal matters, constitutional questions, matters involving non-residents, or disputes exceeding their financial threshold.
§ 3.
Jurisdiction of District Courts
District Courts shall have first-instance jurisdiction over: all criminal matters not reserved to national courts; civil disputes exceeding the Neighborhood Court threshold but not involving constitutional questions; appeals from Neighborhood Courts; inter-Neighborhood disputes; employment, tenancy, and commercial disputes of District scope; and family law matters including inheritance, guardianship, and civil status. District Courts shall have no jurisdiction over constitutional matters, inter-District disputes, or matters expressly reserved to national Courts.
§ 4.
Jurisdiction of National Courts
National Courts shall have first-instance and appellate jurisdiction over: all constitutional matters including Constitutional Review petitions; criminal matters of national gravity including Constitutional Subversion and offences by civic officers in the exercise of their duties; civil disputes of national scope or involving parties from different Districts; appeals from District Courts on questions of law; enforcement of international treaty obligations; and all matters referred by the Guardian Commission or the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate.
§ 5.
Jurisdictional Conflicts
Where a dispute arises as to which Court has jurisdiction, it shall be decided by a panel of three Legal Guides drawn from the Court Registry within seven days. Pending resolution the matter shall be stayed. The decision of the Legal Guide panel is binding on all parties and all Courts. A party who knowingly files in a Court without jurisdiction for the purpose of delay commits an abuse of process and shall be subject to costs and sanctions.
Judicial Philosophy & Procedure
§ 6.
Judicial Philosophy
In the tradition of Athenian democracy, this Republic recognizes that justice is best administered not by a permanent class of professional judges, but by citizens deliberating together. The judiciary of this Republic is founded on the principle that the community, fully informed and properly guided, is the most legitimate arbiter of justice.
§ 6-A.
The Heliast Pool — Standing Jury Reserve
In the tradition of the Athenian Heliaia — which maintained a standing pool of six thousand volunteer Heliasts aged thirty years and over, from whom daily juries were drawn by lot for specific trials — the Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain a Heliast Pool: a standing reserve of citizens who have voluntarily registered for jury service, are aged thirty years and over, have completed the mandatory Civic Education requirement, and have passed a basic Legal Literacy Assessment administered by the Court Registry. The Heliast Pool shall contain no fewer than six thousand registered citizens at all times, drawn from across all Districts and territories of the Republic in proportional representation. Citizens in the Heliast Pool are the primary source from which Citizen Juries in criminal matters, constitutional matters, and high-complexity civil matters are drawn. The Guardian Commission shall maintain the Heliast Pool Registry, publish its composition quarterly, and conduct active outreach to ensure it reflects the full demographic diversity of the Republic.
§ 7.
Citizen Juries
All trials — criminal, civil, and constitutional — shall be decided by Citizen Juries selected by lot from the Civic Registry. Juries for criminal matters shall consist of no fewer than thirty-one citizens. Juries for civil matters shall consist of no fewer than fifteen. Constitutional matters shall be heard by panels of one hundred and one citizens.
§ 8.
The Role of Legal Guides
The Republic shall maintain a corps of Legal Guides — qualified legal professionals who do not decide cases but who educate the jury on applicable law, procedure, and precedent. Legal Guides are civil servants, not advocates for either party. Their role is solely to ensure the jury is fully informed.
§ 9.
No Permanent Judges
No citizen shall hold a permanent judicial office with power to decide the outcome of a case. Administrative judges may be appointed to manage court scheduling, procedure, and the application of settled law in uncontested matters only.
§ 10.
Deliberation and Verdict
After receiving evidence and instruction from Legal Guides, Citizen Juries shall deliberate in private and render a verdict by majority vote. In criminal matters requiring the most severe penalties, a two-thirds supermajority shall be required for conviction.
§ 11.
Appeals
A party may appeal a verdict to a newly selected jury of greater size. No appeal shall be heard by any member of the original jury. The appeal jury shall review the process for procedural error and the verdict for manifest injustice.
§ 12.
Constitutional Review
Any citizen may petition for Constitutional Review of a law they believe to be in conflict with this Constitution. Such petitions shall be heard by a Constitutional Jury of one hundred and one citizens, supported by Legal Guides specializing in constitutional law.
Sentencing & Penalties
§ 13.
Sentencing Guidelines
The Assembly shall by Fundamental Law establish a Sentencing Framework setting the minimum and maximum penalties applicable to each category of offense. Citizen Juries shall determine sentences within the bounds of this Framework. No Citizen Jury may impose a sentence outside the legislated range, except where this Constitution expressly requires a higher threshold for conviction.
§ 14.
Proportionality Principle
In accordance with Aristotle's principle of distributive justice, all sentences shall be proportional to the gravity of the offense, the degree of harm caused, and the circumstances of the offender. Cruel, degrading, or manifestly disproportionate punishments are prohibited. The Sentencing Framework shall be reviewed by the Assembly no less than once every ten years.
§ 15.
Rehabilitation & the Common Good
The purpose of punishment in this Republic is threefold: to protect the community, to restore justice to those harmed, and to rehabilitate the offender so they may return to full civic life. Sentences shall wherever possible include provisions for education, civic reintegration, and restorative engagement with affected communities. Permanent exclusion from civic life shall be reserved only for the gravest offenses against the Republic.
Civil Litigation Procedure
§ 16.
Court Registry
Every District and national Court shall maintain a Court Registry — a permanent administrative office staffed by civil servants responsible for receiving, recording, tracking, and publishing all cases filed within that Court's jurisdiction. The Court Registry shall assign each case a unique identifier upon filing, maintain a publicly accessible case record, and notify all parties of hearing dates and procedural deadlines. No case may proceed without first being registered. The Court Registry shall publish monthly reports on the number, nature, and status of all active cases.
§ 17.
Filing a Lawsuit
Any citizen, group of citizens, organization, or public body with a justiciable claim may file a civil lawsuit by submitting a written Claim of Action to the Court Registry of the appropriate jurisdiction. The Claim of Action shall specify: the identity of the claimant and the respondent; the nature of the harm alleged; the remedy or compensation sought; and the evidence the claimant intends to present. Upon filing, the Court Registry shall serve formal notice upon the respondent within seven days. The respondent shall have thirty days to file a written Response. A Citizen Jury shall then be selected by lot to hear the matter.
§ 18.
Burden of Proof
The standard of proof required shall be determined by the nature of the proceeding: (a) Civil matters shall be decided on the balance of probabilities — that is, the Citizen Jury must be satisfied that the claimant's version of events is more likely true than not; (b) Criminal matters shall be decided beyond reasonable doubt — the Citizen Jury must be firmly satisfied of the accused's guilt before returning a conviction; (c) Constitutional matters shall be decided by clear and convincing evidence — a standard intermediate between civil and criminal. Legal Guides shall instruct juries on the applicable standard before deliberation begins.
§ 19.
Damages & Remedies
Upon finding in favour of a claimant in a civil matter, the Citizen Jury shall determine the remedy, which may include: (a) Compensatory damages — a monetary award restoring the claimant to the position they would have been in absent the harm; (b) Restitutionary damages — recovery of unjust gains made by the respondent at the claimant's expense; (c) Nominal damages — a symbolic award where harm is established but no quantifiable loss is proven; and (d) Punitive damages — an additional award where the respondent's conduct was willfully harmful or grossly negligent, not to exceed three times the compensatory award. The Assembly shall by Fundamental Law establish a Damages Framework to guide jury determinations.
§ 20.
Statute of Limitations
No civil claim may be filed more than six years after the date on which the claimant knew or ought reasonably to have known of the harm. No criminal prosecution may be initiated more than ten years after the date of the alleged offence, except in cases of offences against life, crimes against the Republic, sexual violence, or crimes against children — for which no limitation period shall apply. The Assembly may by Ordinary Law establish specific limitation periods for defined categories of claim, provided these periods are no shorter than those set by this Constitution.
Injunctions, Cease & Desist, and Compulsory Orders
§ 21.
Interim Injunctions
Where a claimant can demonstrate to a Duty Jury of five citizens that: (a) there is a serious question to be tried; (b) immediate and irreparable harm will occur if the order is not granted; and (c) the balance of convenience favours granting the order — the Duty Jury may issue an Interim Injunction prohibiting the respondent from continuing or commencing the action in question pending a full hearing. An Interim Injunction shall take effect immediately upon issuance and shall remain in force for no more than sixty days unless confirmed by a full Citizen Jury hearing.
§ 22.
Cease and Desist Orders
A Cease and Desist Order is a formal directive issued by a Citizen Jury requiring a named person, organization, or public body to immediately stop a specified action that has been found to cause harm, violate the law, or infringe upon the rights of another. Cease and Desist Orders may be issued at the conclusion of a full hearing or as an emergency measure by a Duty Jury pending full proceedings. Any person or body that fails to comply with a Cease and Desist Order within forty-eight hours of service shall be referred immediately to the Courts for contempt proceedings.
§ 23.
Mandatory Orders
Where a Citizen Jury finds that a person or public body has a legal duty to perform a specific act and has failed to do so, it may issue a Mandatory Order compelling performance within a specified timeframe. Mandatory Orders shall be used where monetary compensation alone would be inadequate to remedy the harm. Failure to comply with a Mandatory Order constitutes contempt of court. Public bodies subject to Mandatory Orders shall report compliance to the Court Registry within the specified timeframe, failing which the matter shall be escalated to the Council for enforcement.
Subpoenas & Evidence
§ 24.
Judicial Subpoena Power
Any Citizen Jury presiding over a matter shall have the power to issue subpoenas compelling: (a) the attendance and testimony of any person within the jurisdiction of the Republic, whether as a witness or as a party to proceedings; and (b) the production of documents, records, data, or physical evidence in the possession of any person or body, public or private. Subpoenas shall be issued through the Court Registry and served formally upon the recipient. No person may be compelled to give testimony that would directly incriminate themselves; all other grounds for refusal must be established before the Jury and are subject to its determination.
§ 25.
Contempt of Court
Any person or body that wilfully disobeys a lawful order of a Citizen Jury — including a subpoena, Cease and Desist Order, Interim Injunction, or Mandatory Order — commits contempt of court. Contempt shall be tried before a fresh Citizen Jury of fifteen citizens. Upon a finding of contempt, the Jury may impose: daily financial penalties until compliance is achieved; in cases of individual contempt, a custodial sentence not exceeding ninety days; and in cases of contempt by a public official, referral for impeachment proceedings. No fine or penalty for contempt may be waived by any party other than the Court.
§ 26.
Rules of Evidence
The Assembly shall by Fundamental Law establish a Code of Evidence governing the admissibility, weight, and presentation of evidence before Citizen Juries. Until such Code is enacted, the following principles shall apply: evidence obtained through torture, unlawful search, or coercion is inadmissible in all proceedings; hearsay evidence may be admitted at the Jury's discretion with appropriate caution; expert evidence shall be presented through court-appointed neutral experts, not partisan witnesses; and all evidence presented to the Jury shall be made available to both parties in advance of the hearing.
Legal Representation
§ 27.
Right to Representation
Every party to a proceeding before any Court of this Republic has the right to be represented by a qualified legal advocate of their choosing. The Republic shall maintain a Public Advocates Office — a body of professional legal advocates available at no cost to any citizen who cannot afford private representation. No proceeding before a Citizen Jury shall commence without the Court Registry confirming that all parties have been given a reasonable opportunity to secure representation. The right to self-representation is preserved; however, Legal Guides shall ensure that a self-represented party understands the process before proceedings begin.
§ 28.
Role of Private Advocates vs. Legal Guides
Private legal advocates represent the interests of their client and may argue on their client's behalf before the Jury. Legal Guides serve the Jury itself — they are neutral officers of the Court whose duty is to ensure the Jury understands the law, not to advocate for either party. No Legal Guide may simultaneously serve as a private advocate in any proceeding. The distinction between advocacy and legal guidance shall be clearly explained to every Citizen Jury before a hearing commences.
Jury Impartiality & Anti-Bias Protections
§ 29.
Jury Disclosure Requirement
Every citizen selected by lot to serve on a Citizen Jury shall, before the commencement of proceedings, complete a sworn Disclosure Declaration administered by the Court Registry. The Declaration shall require the prospective juror to disclose: any personal, familial, financial, or professional relationship with any party, witness, or advocate involved in the matter; any prior knowledge of or formed opinion about the specific case; any membership in an organization with a direct interest in the outcome; and any other circumstance that a reasonable person would consider capable of affecting impartial judgment. Knowingly providing false information in a Disclosure Declaration constitutes perjury.
§ 30.
Jury Challenge Process
Upon reviewing Disclosure Declarations, each party to a proceeding shall have the right to challenge the inclusion of any prospective juror on grounds of demonstrated bias or conflict of interest. Challenges shall be of two kinds: (a) Challenge for Cause — an unlimited right to seek removal of any juror where a specific, articulable reason for bias is demonstrated; and (b) Peremptory Challenge — a limited right, not exceeding three per party per proceeding, to remove a prospective juror without stating a reason. Peremptory Challenges may not be exercised in a pattern that systematically excludes citizens on the basis of ethnicity, gender, religion, or socioeconomic class; any such pattern shall invalidate the challenge and be referred to the Guardian Commission for investigation.
§ 31.
Stratified Jury Composition
To prevent group bias arising from homogeneous jury composition, all Citizen Juries of fifteen members or more shall be stratified by the Guardian Commission to reflect, as closely as practicable, the demographic diversity of the Republic in terms of: geographic region, including both urban and rural communities; age distribution; gender balance; and socioeconomic background. No jury shall be drawn exclusively from the same Neighborhood or District as either party to the proceedings. In cases involving alleged discrimination against a minority community, the jury shall include a proportional representation of members of that community.
§ 32.
Mandatory Bias Awareness Instruction
Before the commencement of every trial, Legal Guides shall deliver a mandatory Bias Awareness Instruction to the full Citizen Jury. This instruction shall: explain the nature of both conscious and unconscious bias and how it may affect judgment; identify common categories of implicit bias, including assumptions based on wealth, appearance, ethnicity, and gender; instruct jurors to base their deliberations solely on the evidence presented in court; and require each juror to affirm, by signed declaration, that they understand and commit to impartial deliberation. The content and delivery of the Bias Awareness Instruction shall be standardized by the Assembly through Fundamental Law and reviewed no less than every five years.
§ 33.
Secret Ballot Deliberation
All votes within a Citizen Jury during deliberation shall be conducted by secret ballot. No juror may be compelled, pressured, or observed when casting their deliberative vote. The use of secret ballots shall protect jurors from social coercion, groupthink, and the undue influence of dominant personalities within the jury room. Deliberations shall begin with an anonymous preliminary vote to establish the initial distribution of opinion before any discussion occurs, preventing anchoring bias from vocal early declarations. The final verdict vote shall likewise be by secret ballot. Only the final verdict — not individual votes — shall be disclosed publicly.
§ 34.
Sequestration in High-Profile Cases
In cases of exceptional public prominence — as determined by a Duty Jury of five citizens upon application by any party — the Citizen Jury shall be sequestered for the duration of the trial. Sequestration shall involve: accommodation of jurors in neutral, state-provided facilities separate from their ordinary environment; prohibition on access to news media, social media, and public commentary relating to the case; supervised communication with family members limited to personal welfare matters only; and a media blackout order prohibiting any publication of prejudicial commentary about the case while the jury is seated. Violation of a media blackout order shall constitute contempt of court.
§ 35.
Prohibition on Jury Tampering
Jury tampering — defined as any attempt, direct or indirect, to influence the opinion, vote, or conduct of a juror through bribery, threat, deception, unauthorized communication, or any other improper means — is among the gravest offences against the justice of this Republic. Any person convicted of jury tampering shall face: permanent disqualification from civic participation; a custodial sentence as prescribed by the Sentencing Framework; and, where the tampering resulted in a corrupt verdict, the automatic retrial of the original matter before a freshly selected jury. Any juror who receives an approach that may constitute tampering is obligated to report it immediately to the Court Registry under penalty of contempt.
§ 36.
Post-Verdict Bias Review
Any party to a proceeding who has reasonable grounds to believe that a verdict was materially affected by juror bias, tampering, or failure to follow the Bias Awareness Instruction may petition the Court Registry for a Post-Verdict Bias Review within thirty days of the verdict. Such petitions shall be assessed by a Review Panel of three Legal Guides who shall determine whether the alleged bias was sufficiently material to have affected the outcome. Where the Review Panel finds credible evidence of material bias, the matter shall be referred to a fresh Citizen Jury for retrial, and the affected juror referred to the Courts for contempt or perjury proceedings as appropriate.
Judicial Independence
§ 37.
Declaration of Judicial Independence
The Tribunal Court of the Dikasteria — at every level, from Neighborhood Courts to National Courts — are hereby declared fully and permanently independent of the Assembly, the Council, all Departments, all Agencies, and all other institutions of the Grand Equestrian Republic. No branch, body, officer, or citizen of the Republic may direct, influence, pressure, obstruct, or interfere with the deliberations, verdicts, or administration of any Court or Citizen Jury. Judicial independence is not a privilege of the Courts — it is a right of every citizen who comes before them.
§ 38.
Guaranteed Judicial Budget
The budget of the Courts is constitutionally guaranteed. The judicial budget shall not fall below the prior year's appropriation without the approval of a two-thirds supermajority of the full Assembly and a concurrent finding by a Constitutional Jury that the reduction is not motivated by a desire to weaken judicial function. Any Assembly member who advocates for judicial budget reduction with the purpose of influencing a pending case commits an offence against the constitutional order.
§ 39.
Protection of Legal Guides
Legal Guides are officers of the Courts, not of the Assembly or the Council. No Department, Agency, or official may issue instructions to any Legal Guide concerning their legal education to a Citizen Jury. A Legal Guide who receives an improper instruction is obligated to report it to the Court Registry and the Guardian Commission within twenty-four hours. Issuing such an instruction constitutes judicial interference and shall be prosecuted accordingly.
§ 40.
Non-Interference Clause
The following acts are declared offences of judicial interference: (a) publicly commenting on guilt or innocence of a party in a pending case to influence the jury; (b) passing any law designed to reverse a specific court verdict already rendered; (c) directing Legal Guides, the Court Registry, or any juror in a specific case; (d) withholding information from a Court in defiance of a lawful subpoena; (e) threatening or retaliating against any juror, Legal Guide, or Court Registry officer; and (f) using emergency powers to delay or suspend any court proceeding. Each act shall result upon conviction in permanent disqualification from civic office.
§ 41.
The Judicial Rules Commission
There is hereby established the Judicial Rules Commission — an independent body of twenty-one citizens selected by lot — responsible for drafting, maintaining, and updating the Code of Evidence, the Sentencing Framework, the Damages Framework, and all procedural rules governing the Courts. The Assembly may enact the Commission's recommendations as Fundamental Law but may not amend individual provisions to influence a specific pending case. All proposed amendments must be reviewed by the Commission before Assembly consideration, and the Commission's opinion shall be published alongside any Assembly vote.
§ 42.
Prospective Application of Rule Changes
No change to the Code of Evidence, Sentencing Framework, Damages Framework, or any procedural rule shall apply retroactively to any case already filed, verdict already rendered, or sentence already imposed. All judicial rule changes apply prospectively only. Any law purporting to apply judicial rule changes retroactively is void from the moment of its enactment and shall be referred immediately to a Constitutional Jury for review.
§ 43.
Separation of Judicial Administration
The day-to-day administration of the Courts shall be managed by the Chief Court Administrator — a professional civil servant appointed by the Judicial Rules Commission for a fixed five-year term — who answers to the Commission and to no other body. The Assembly and the Council shall have no role in the appointment, direction, or dismissal of the Chief Court Administrator. Removal may only be effected by a two-thirds vote of the Commission following a Citizen Jury finding of gross misconduct.
§ 44.
The Courts May Review the Assembly and Council
The Courts shall have full power of constitutional review over all acts of the Assembly and the Council. No law or directive is immune from Constitutional Review upon petition by any citizen with standing. A finding of unconstitutionality renders the impugned act void from the date of the finding. Neither the Assembly nor the Council may reinstate a law or directive found unconstitutional without first amending the constitutional provision violated, through the process prescribed in Article XII.
§ 45.
The Assembly and Council May Not Review the Courts
Neither the Assembly nor the Council may review, reverse, overrule, or annul any verdict, order, or ruling of any Court. The Assembly may legislate generally for the future; it may not legislate specifically against a past verdict. Any law specifically designed to nullify a named court verdict is void and constitutes judicial interference.
§ 46.
Mutual Respect of Constitutional Roles
The Assembly makes law. The Council administers law. The Courts apply law to the facts before them. Each branch shall respect the constitutional role of the others. Where genuine uncertainty exists about the boundary between legislative, executive, and judicial authority, the matter shall be referred to a Constitutional Jury for a binding ruling. The three branches are co-equal in dignity, distinct in function, and mutually accountable. No branch is master of the others — all three serve the citizens from whom their authority derives.

Article VI
⚖
The Areopagus — Tribunal of Grave Offences
In Athens, the Areopagus was the ancient tribunal of last resort — composed of those who had served and could not be dismissed. The Grand Equestrian Republic restores this principle: a tribunal of experienced civic servants for the Republic's gravest cases.
Establishment & Composition
§ 1.
Establishment
There is hereby established the Areopagus of the Grand Equestrian Republic — a standing tribunal of civic experience, composed exclusively of citizens who have completed at least one full term of service in the Assembly, the Council, the Guardian Commission, the Office of the Public Auditor, or the Referendum Commission, and who have been found to have fulfilled their civic duty with honour as certified by the end-of-term scrutiny process of Article VII. The Areopagus is not a court of first instance — it is a court of gravity, convened only for the most serious categories of offence enumerated in this Article.
§ 2.
Membership
Upon the honourable conclusion of civic service and confirmation by end-of-term scrutiny, eligible citizens are automatically enrolled in the Areopagus Registry maintained by the Guardian Commission. Membership is permanent and may only be revoked by a finding of misconduct by a Constitutional Jury. From the Areopagus Registry, juries of one hundred and one members shall be selected by lot for each case brought before the Areopagus. No citizen serving concurrently in any other civic office may sit on an Areopagus jury. The Areopagus Registry shall be published in full in the Official Gazette annually.
Jurisdiction
§ 3.
Exclusive Jurisdiction
The Areopagus shall have exclusive first-instance jurisdiction over the following categories of case: (a) Constitutional Subversion under Article XV §1 — the gravest offence against the Republic; (b) deliberate homicide, attempted homicide, and the infliction of grievous bodily harm with intent; (c) arson of public buildings, sacred spaces, and civic infrastructure; (d) poisoning resulting in death or grievous harm; (e) treason and espionage against the Grand Equestrian Republic; (f) Civic Oath breach under Article II §8 where the breach has caused material harm to the constitutional order; and (g) crimes against the Civic Registry, the Oath Registry, or the Constitutional Precedent Registry. These categories are reserved to the Areopagus because they strike at the foundations of civic life itself and require the judgment of those who have demonstrated the deepest commitment to the constitutional order.
§ 4.
Procedure
Cases before the Areopagus shall be prosecuted by the Department of Justice and heard before a jury of one hundred and one members selected from the Areopagus Registry by lot. Legal Guides shall provide the jury with instruction on the applicable law. The evidentiary standard shall be beyond reasonable doubt. Conviction requires a two-thirds supermajority of the one hundred and one sitting members. Sentences shall be determined within the Sentencing Framework established by the Assembly and shall reflect the gravity of offences reserved to the Areopagus. Appeals from the Areopagus shall be heard by a fresh Areopagus jury of one hundred and fifty-one members, selected by lot from the Registry, and shall succeed only upon a finding of procedural error or manifest injustice by a two-thirds majority.

Article VII
🏛
The Archons — Senior Civic Officers
Athens governed its civic life through named senior officers — the Archons — each with defined jurisdictional responsibilities. The Grand Equestrian Republic restores this principle: named senior civic officers with clear mandates, chosen by lot, accountable to the people.
The Four Archons
§ 1.
Establishment of the Archons
The Grand Equestrian Republic hereby establishes four Archons — senior civic officers selected by lot from the Civic Registry, each serving a one-year non-renewable term, each with defined jurisdictional responsibilities within the civic administration of the Republic. The Archons are not political officers; they are administrative magistrates whose function is to ensure the smooth, lawful, and equitable operation of specific domains of civic life. Each Archon shall swear the Civic Oath upon appointment and shall be subject to end-of-term scrutiny under Article VII upon the conclusion of their term.
§ 2.
The Civic Archon
The Civic Archon — successor to the Athenian Eponymous Archon — shall: organize and oversee the Republic's public civic celebrations, national days, and commemorative events; preside over the Court Registry in matters of family law including inheritance disputes, guardianship proceedings, and civil status determinations; serve as the official custodian of the Republic's civic calendar; and act as the formal point of reception for visiting foreign civic delegations in the absence of a designated diplomatic officer. The Civic Archon shall maintain a public office accessible to any citizen requiring guidance on civil status matters and shall publish a monthly Civic Register of all significant civil status determinations made under their jurisdiction.
§ 3.
The Defense Archon
The Defense Archon — successor to the Athenian Polemarch Archon — shall: liaise between the Council and the E.U.P.B.H. on administrative and logistical matters; oversee the welfare, pensions, and civic reintegration of veterans of the Defense Forces; preside over matters relating to the civic rights and obligations of non-citizen residents of the Republic in matters affecting defense and public order; and coordinate the administrative interface between the Department of Defense and the constitutional institutions during declared emergencies. The Defense Archon is a civilian administrative officer and holds no military command authority.
§ 4.
The Sacred Archon
The Sacred Archon — successor to the Athenian King Archon — shall: oversee the administration of the Republic's recognized cultural and spiritual heritage festivals in their civic dimension, without exercising any religious authority; preside over the Court Registry in matters of inheritance law where cultural or traditional practices are relevant to the determination; serve as the custodian of the Republic's ancient customary practices and traditions as catalogued by the Department of Education and Culture; and ensure that the civic ceremonies of the Republic — including the swearing of Civic Oaths — are conducted with proper dignity and constitutional form. The Sacred Archon holds no religious authority and may not make determinations based on religious doctrine.
§ 5.
The Thesmothetai — Six Law Archons
The Thesmothetai — six Law Archons selected by lot — shall collectively: preside over the Court Registry in matters of administrative law, regulatory compliance, and the examination of civic officers' conduct; review the legality of decrees and directives issued by the Council to ensure consistency with Assembly-enacted law; manage the scheduling, procedural administration, and case assignment of the National Courts; maintain the official compilation of the Republic's laws and ensure it is accurate, accessible, and updated within thirty days of any legislative change; and refer to the Constitutional Precedent Registry any case that appears to raise a novel constitutional question. The Thesmothetai shall meet weekly and publish a monthly report on the state of the judicial caseload and any legal anomalies identified in their review of Council decrees.
Civil Magistracies
§ 6.
The Civil Magistracy System
In the tradition of the Athenian civil magistracy system — which maintained over six hundred civil magistrates covering every domain of public administration — the Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain a system of Civil Magistrates: citizens selected by lot from the Civic Registry, aged thirty years and over, to serve fixed one-year terms in specific administrative functions across the Republic. Civil Magistrates are the operational layer of civic administration — the citizens who actually run the day-to-day functions of the Republic between the level of Department Director-General and the individual citizen. Every Civil Magistrate is subject to the Dokimasia scrutiny of the Public Auditor at the end of their term. No citizen may serve in the same Civil Magistracy in two consecutive terms.
§ 7.
The Superintendent of Public Works & Infrastructure
The Republic shall maintain a Superintendent of Public Works and Infrastructure — a Civil Magistrate selected by lot — responsible for overseeing the maintenance, repair, and operational integrity of public infrastructure within their assigned jurisdiction, including roads, bridges, public buildings, water systems, and community facilities. Superintendents shall be appointed at the District level, with one per District. They report to the Department of Infrastructure and Environment and to the District Council. The office of Superintendent of Springs — the Athenian magistracy responsible for water supply — is preserved in spirit through this provision, recognizing that public water, sanitation, and infrastructure are constitutional obligations of the first order.
§ 8.
The Commissioners of the Civic Fund
In the tradition of the Athenian Commissioners of the Theoric Fund — who administered public funds distributed to citizens to enable their civic participation — the Republic shall maintain Commissioners of the Civic Fund in each District: Civil Magistrates selected by lot, responsible for administering the civic participation stipends, jury compensation, assembly member compensation, and other public funds designed to ensure that no citizen is financially excluded from civic life. The Commissioners of the Civic Fund report to the Public Auditor and the Department of the Treasury jointly. They are responsible for the accuracy, fairness, and accessibility of civic fund distributions and shall publish quarterly accounts in the Official Gazette.
§ 9.
The Six Hundred — The General Civil Magistracy Corps
Beyond the named magistracies, the Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain a General Civil Magistracy Corps — a body of no fewer than six hundred Civil Magistrates selected by lot across all Districts and territories, serving one-year terms in specific administrative functions as defined by Fundamental Law. The Corps covers the full range of civic administration including: public market oversight; civic record maintenance; community dispute mediation; public health inspection; environmental monitoring; civic education facilitation; census administration; and any other administrative function the Assembly by Ordinary Law assigns to citizen magistrates rather than professional civil servants. The six-hundred minimum ensures that civic administration remains in the hands of ordinary citizens rather than drifting entirely into a professional bureaucracy. The Guardian Commission shall maintain a Magistracy Register listing all current Civil Magistrate positions, their incumbents, their terms, and their Dokimasia outcomes.

Article VIII
🛡
The Guardian Commission
§ 1.
Establishment
There is hereby established an independent Guardian Commission, responsible for maintaining the integrity of the Civic Registry, administering all lottery selections, and serving as the custodian of this Constitution.
§ 2.
Composition
The Guardian Commission shall consist of fifteen citizens selected by lot, serving staggered three-year terms. Members of the Guardian Commission are ineligible for any other civic office during and for five years following their service.
§ 3.
Powers and Duties
The Guardian Commission shall: verify and maintain the Civic Registry; conduct all civic lotteries with full public transparency; investigate allegations of corruption or registry manipulation; publish annual reports on the health of the democratic system; and refer suspected violations to the Courts.
§ 4.
Independence
The Guardian Commission is not subject to the direction of the Assembly or the Council. Its budget is constitutionally guaranteed and may not be reduced below its prior year appropriation without a three-fourths vote of the Assembly.

Article IX
📊
The Office of the Public Auditor
Public money belongs to the public. Every coin spent in the name of this Republic shall be accounted for, verified, and disclosed — without exception.
§ 1.
Establishment
There is hereby established the Office of the Public Auditor, an independent institution responsible for examining, verifying, and publicly reporting on all expenditure of public funds by every branch, ministry, commission, and agency of the Republic. The Office operates independently of the Assembly, the Council, and the Guardian Commission.
§ 2.
Composition
The Office of the Public Auditor shall be led by a Board of Auditors consisting of nine citizens selected by lot from the Civic Registry, serving staggered four-year terms. Candidates must have demonstrated competency in financial administration, accounting, or public administration, verified by an independent expert panel. No Board member may hold any other civic office during or for six years following their service.
§ 3.
Powers & Duties
The Office of the Public Auditor shall: conduct annual audits of all public accounts and publish findings in full within the Official Gazette; investigate any allegation of financial mismanagement, waste, or fraud; subpoena financial records from any public body; refer evidence of criminal conduct to the Courts; and present an annual State of the Treasury report to the Assembly and the public. No public body may withhold financial records from the Office.
§ 4.
Independence & Funding
The budget of the Office of the Public Auditor is constitutionally guaranteed and may not be reduced by any act of the Assembly or Council. Any attempt to interfere with, obstruct, or defund the Office constitutes the crime of civic sabotage, punishable by permanent disqualification from all public service.
End-of-Term Scrutiny (Dokimasia)
§ 5.
The Scrutiny Process
In the tradition of the Athenian dokimasia — the formal end-of-term examination of all civic officers — every citizen who has served in any civic office of the Grand Equestrian Republic shall, within sixty days of the conclusion of their term, submit to a formal End-of-Term Scrutiny conducted by the Office of the Public Auditor. The Scrutiny shall examine: whether the officer fulfilled all constitutional obligations of their office; whether any public funds under their administration were properly accounted for; whether the officer made any decisions that materially benefited themselves, their family, or any private organization at the public expense; whether the officer adhered to the Civic Oath throughout their term; and whether the officer committed any act during their term that constitutes a breach of the Constitution. Every officer — from Citizen Juror to Council member, from Legal Guide to Guardian Commissioner — shall be subject to the Scrutiny without exception.
§ 6.
Scrutiny Before the Popular Tribunal
The findings of the End-of-Term Scrutiny shall be published in the Official Gazette within fourteen days of completion. Any citizen who believes that an officer's Scrutiny reveals evidence of misconduct, constitutional violation, or breach of the Civic Oath may, within thirty days of publication, file a formal complaint before the Courts — the Popular Tribunal of the Republic — citing the specific findings of the Scrutiny. The officer being scrutinized has the right to respond in full to any complaint filed. A Citizen Jury of thirty-one persons shall then hear the complaint and the officer's response and determine by simple majority whether further proceedings are warranted. This mechanism gives every citizen the constitutional standing to hold every civic officer to account at the end of their service — not merely during it.
§ 7.
Scrutiny Records & the Civic Service Registry
The Office of the Public Auditor shall maintain a permanent Civic Service Registry — a public record of every citizen who has served in any civic office, the office held, the term of service, and the outcome of their End-of-Term Scrutiny. The Registry shall be publicly accessible at all times and shall be updated within thirty days of the completion of each Scrutiny. The Civic Service Registry is the constitutional memory of civic service in the Grand Equestrian Republic — it honours those who served faithfully and ensures that those who abused their office cannot escape the public record. No civic officer may hold subsequent civic office until their End-of-Term Scrutiny for their prior office is complete and any outstanding complaints resolved.

The Referendum Commission
A referendum is the voice of the people made law. Its integrity must be beyond question — protected from manipulation, misinformation, and the influence of wealth.
§ 1.
Establishment
There is hereby established the Referendum Commission, an independent body responsible for administering all referenda, citizen veto votes, and constitutional ratification votes conducted under this Constitution. The Commission operates independently of all other branches and commissions.
§ 2.
Composition
The Referendum Commission shall consist of eleven citizens selected by lot from the Civic Registry, serving staggered three-year terms. Members must not have held any civic office within the preceding five years. The Commission shall be supported by a permanent professional secretariat of civil servants expert in electoral administration.
§ 3.
Powers & Duties
The Referendum Commission shall: design and administer all referendum ballots with clarity and impartiality; establish a mandatory public information period of no fewer than thirty days before any referendum vote; ensure equal access to civic information for all citizens; investigate and refer to the Courts any attempt to spread deliberate misinformation regarding a referendum question; publish all results with full statistical transparency; and certify or invalidate referendum outcomes based on procedural compliance.
§ 4.
Campaign Integrity
No person, organisation, or foreign entity may spend money to influence a referendum outcome. Civic debate on referendum questions shall occur through publicly funded, equally accessible forums only. Paid advertising for or against any referendum question is prohibited. Violation of these provisions constitutes the crime of democratic interference, punishable by severe civil penalty and permanent disqualification from civic participation.

Prohibition on Political Money

Article XII
⚔
The Defense Forces of the Republic
The defense forces exist to protect the Republic and its citizens — never to govern them. The sword is always subordinate to the common good.
§ 1.
Establishment & Purpose
The Republic shall maintain a professional Defense Force whose sole purpose is the protection of the Republic's territory, citizens, and constitutional order from external threat. The Defense Force is a servant of the Republic, not its master. It shall never be used as an instrument of political power, domestic suppression, or personal authority.
§ 2.
Civilian Command
The Defense Force shall at all times be under the collective civilian command of the Council, accountable to the Assembly. No military officer may issue orders that violate this Constitution or contradict the lawful direction of the Council. Any military officer who follows an unconstitutional order bears personal criminal liability. The highest military rank is a professional administrative role — it confers no political authority whatsoever.
§ 3.
Prohibition on Domestic Deployment
The Defense Force may not be deployed against the citizens of the Republic under any circumstance. Domestic public order is the exclusive responsibility of civil law enforcement, which shall be governed by separate legislation and remain fully subordinate to civilian judicial oversight. Any order to deploy the Defense Force domestically is void and constitutes the gravest crime against the Republic.
§ 4.
Budget & Transparency
The defense budget shall be set annually by the Assembly and shall not exceed a ceiling established by Fundamental Law without a two-thirds Assembly vote. All defense expenditure is subject to audit by the Office of the Public Auditor. No secret military fund may be maintained outside the official defense budget. Defense procurement contracts shall be published in the Official Gazette within ninety days of signing.
§ 5.
Conscription
The Republic shall maintain a voluntary professional Defense Force in peacetime. Conscription may only be introduced by the Assembly by Fundamental Law vote during a formally declared state of war, and only for the duration of that declared conflict. Conscientious objectors shall be offered alternative civic service of equivalent duration and obligation.
Military Magistracies
§ 6.
The Ten Generals (Strategoi)
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain ten Generals — the Strategoi — as the senior military commanders of the Defense Forces. Unlike most civic offices, the Generals shall be appointed on the basis of demonstrated military competency, verified by an independent expert panel, and confirmed by the Council. Each General commands a designated regional division of the Defense Forces corresponding to the Republic's ten principal regions. The Generals collectively form the High Military Council, which advises the Council on defense matters and coordinates the operational conduct of the Defense Forces. The Generals are subject to the Council's civilian oversight and may be removed by two-thirds Council vote. They are subject to the Dokimasia scrutiny of the Public Auditor at the end of each term. The Generals shall preside over the Heliaia in all affairs relative to the duties, conduct, and faults of the military, in the tradition of the Athenian Strategoi.
§ 7.
The Ten Taxiarchs — Infantry Commanders
Below the Generals, each of the Republic's ten regional divisions shall be commanded by a Taxiarch — a senior infantry officer responsible for the organization, training, and operational readiness of the ground forces within their region. Taxiarchs shall be appointed from the professional officer corps on the basis of merit and confirmed by the relevant General. They report to their regional General and to the Department of Defense. There shall be ten Taxiarchs in total, one per regional division, mirroring the Athenian practice of one Taxiarch per tribe. Taxiarchs are subject to end-of-term Dokimasia by the Public Auditor.
§ 8.
The Ten Phylarchs — Cavalry & Aerial Commanders
Each regional division shall also maintain a Phylarch — a senior officer commanding the cavalry, aerial, and rapid-response forces of that region. In the Equestrian Republic, Phylarchs command formations that include Pegasus cavalry units, unicorn tactical units, and other specialized mobile forces, in addition to conventional mounted and aerial elements. There shall be ten Phylarchs in total, one per regional division. Phylarchs are appointed by merit, confirmed by their regional General, and subject to Dokimasia at the end of their term.
§ 9.
The Three Hipparchs — Senior Cavalry Command
Above the Phylarchs, the Republic shall maintain three Hipparchs — senior cavalry and mobile forces commanders with national-level authority over all Phylarch commands. The Hipparchs coordinate the Republic's cavalry, aerial, and rapid deployment forces across regional boundaries, ensuring national operational coherence. They are appointed by the High Military Council and confirmed by the Council, serve fixed two-year terms, and are subject to Dokimasia upon conclusion of each term. The three Hipparchs shall include representation from the three major geographic zones of the Republic: the continental heartland, the coastal territories, and the autonomous regions.
§ 10.
The Military Treasurer
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain a Military Treasurer — a civilian officer appointed by the Council from a pool of candidates with demonstrated expertise in public finance, serving a fixed three-year term. The Military Treasurer is responsible for: administering the defense budget as authorized by the Assembly; auditing all military expenditure and reporting quarterly to both the Council and the Public Auditor; detecting and referring any instance of financial irregularity, procurement fraud, or misappropriation of defense funds to the Courts; and ensuring that no defense expenditure occurs outside the constitutionally authorized budget. The Military Treasurer is a civilian, not a military officer, and may not be directed by the Generals, Taxiarchs, Phylarchs, or Hipparchs. The Military Treasurer answers to the Council and the Public Auditor jointly.

Article XIII
🏛
The Structure of the State
Aristotle taught that the best state is one sized for genuine self-governance — large enough for self-sufficiency, small enough that citizens may know one another and participate meaningfully in public life.
Tier One — The Neighborhood (Geitonia)
§ 1.
The Neighborhood as the Foundation of Democracy
In the spirit of Aristotle's teaching that the polis is the natural home of the political animal, the most fundamental unit of governance in the Grand Equestrian Republic is the Neighborhood — known by its ancient name, the Geitonia. A Neighborhood is a community small enough that its citizens may know one another, walk its streets together, and deliberate face-to-face on matters of common concern. No Neighborhood shall have fewer than five hundred nor more than five thousand registered citizens.
§ 2.
Neighborhood Assembly & Council
Every Neighborhood shall govern itself through a Neighborhood Assembly of fifty citizens selected by lot from its registered residents, serving one-year terms; and a Neighborhood Council of nine citizens selected by lot, serving six-month terms. The Neighborhood Assembly shall decide all matters of purely local concern: public spaces, local roads, community facilities, neighborhood safety, and local cultural life. All sessions of the Neighborhood Assembly shall be open to every resident to attend and speak, though only selected members may vote.
§ 3.
Neighborhood Courts
Each Neighborhood shall maintain a Neighborhood Court of eleven citizen jurors selected by lot to resolve minor civil disputes, neighborhood infractions, and matters of local custom. Neighborhood Courts may not impose custodial sentences. Appeals from Neighborhood Courts shall be heard by the District Courts. Neighborhood Courts shall embody the Aristotelian ideal of justice administered by one's peers — citizens who know the community and share its life.
§ 4.
Neighborhood Boundaries
Neighborhood boundaries shall be drawn to reflect genuine communities — defined by shared streets, markets, schools, and civic spaces — rather than administrative convenience. Boundaries shall be set by the District Boundaries Commission following community consultation, and may be revised no more than once every five years. No Neighborhood boundary shall divide a recognizable community of shared life without the consent of the majority of its residents.
Tier Two — The District (Demos)
§ 5.
The District as Regional Government
Above the Neighborhood and below the national government sits the District — known as the Demos, recalling the Athenian unit of local citizenship from which democracy takes its very name. A District is composed of no fewer than ten and no more than fifty Neighborhoods, forming a regional community large enough for economic self-sufficiency, shared infrastructure, and regional planning. Each District shall govern itself through its own Assembly, Council, and Courts, constituted by lot on the same principles as the national bodies.
§ 6.
District Assembly & Council
The District Assembly shall be composed of three hundred citizens selected by lot from all Neighborhoods within the District, proportionally stratified by Neighborhood population. It shall govern matters of regional concern: regional roads and transport, district hospitals and schools, regional environmental management, inter-Neighborhood disputes, and district-level economic policy. The District Council shall be composed of thirty citizens selected by lot, serving one-year terms, responsible for day-to-day regional administration.
§ 7.
District Boundaries
District boundaries shall be established and revised by a national Boundaries Commission of twenty-one citizens selected by lot, convened no more than once every ten years following a national census. Boundaries shall be drawn on the basis of geography, economic interdependence, shared infrastructure, and cultural affinity. No District boundary shall be drawn to advantage any class, community, or interest over another. All boundary determinations are subject to Constitutional Review by a Constitutional Jury.
The Three-Tier Principle
§ 8.
Subsidiarity
The Grand Equestrian Republic is governed on the principle of subsidiarity — that every matter shall be decided at the lowest tier of government capable of handling it justly and effectively. Neighborhoods govern what is local. Districts govern what is regional. The national bodies govern only what cannot be handled below. No higher tier of government shall assume a power appropriately exercised by a lower tier. Where doubt exists as to the appropriate tier, the matter shall be resolved in favour of the lower tier.
§ 9.
Inter-Tier Disputes
Disputes between a Neighborhood and its District, or between a District and the national government, over the proper allocation of powers shall be resolved by a Constitutional Jury of one hundred and one citizens, drawn equally from the tiers in dispute. No higher tier of government may override a lower tier's decision on a matter clearly within the lower tier's jurisdiction without a ruling from a Constitutional Jury. This protection of local self-governance is fundamental to the character of the Republic.
§ 10.
The Middle Class as Stabilizer
Aristotle identified the middling class as the anchor of stable democracy. The Republic shall therefore maintain policies that actively prevent the excessive concentration of wealth or the entrenchment of poverty: progressive contribution to public funds based on capacity; universal access to education and healthcare; and regular review by the Assembly of economic conditions to ensure no class of citizens is systematically excluded from civic life by material deprivation.
Civic Education & Character
§ 11.
Education for Citizenship
Aristotle held that the character of citizens shapes the character of the state. The Republic shall therefore provide every citizen, from youth, with an education in: the history and philosophy of democracy; the structure and operation of this Constitution; the art of reasoned argument and civic deliberation; and the duties and privileges of civic participation. No citizen shall be selected for civic service without access to this foundation.
§ 12.
The Civic Virtues
The Republic recognizes, following Aristotle, that democracy requires virtuous citizens — not merely compliant ones. The state shall cultivate through education, public culture, and example the following civic virtues: prudence in deliberation; justice in dealings with fellow citizens; courage in speaking truth to power; temperance in the exercise of authority; and magnanimity in service to the common good. These virtues are not enforced by law but nurtured by institution.
§ 13.
The Mixed Constitution
Aristotle's highest political ideal was the mixed constitution — one that draws from the strengths of democracy, aristocracy of merit, and rule of law, while correcting for the excesses of each. This Constitution embodies that ideal: the lottery prevents oligarchy; the deliberation requirement tempers mob passion; the rule of law constrains arbitrary power; and the rotation of offices ensures no virtue of birth or wealth confers permanent authority. The Republic is not a pure democracy — it is a just one.
State Identity
§ 14.
Capital & Seat of Government
The capital of the Grand Equestrian Republic and the seat of all national government bodies shall be designated by Fundamental Law of the Assembly. The capital shall be a free civic city, governed by its own Polity assembly, and shall not be subject to the exclusive control of any branch of the national government. The Assembly, the Council, and all national commissions shall maintain their principal offices within the capital.
§ 15.
Polity Boundaries
The boundaries of each Polity shall be established and revised by a Boundaries Commission of twenty-one citizens selected by lot, convened no more than once every ten years following a national census. Boundary decisions shall be made on the basis of geography, community of interest, and population size, and shall not be drawn to advantage any group, class, or community over another. All boundary determinations are subject to Constitutional Review.
§ 16.
National Symbols & Language
The official language of the Republic for purposes of governance, legislation, and civic administration shall be designated by Fundamental Law. The Republic shall protect and honour all languages spoken within its borders as expressions of cultural heritage. The national flag, seal, and anthem shall be established by Fundamental Law. No symbol of the Republic shall incorporate imagery associated with any single religion, ethnicity, political faction, or dynasty.
State of Emergency
§ 17.
Declaration of Emergency
A state of national emergency may be declared only upon the occurrence of an event posing an imminent, severe, and extraordinary threat to the life, safety, or constitutional order of the Republic — including natural disaster, pandemic, foreign attack, or active constitutional subversion — and only by the following process: an absolute majority of the full Council shall pass an Emergency Declaration specifying the nature and geographic scope of the threat; the specific rights proposed for temporary limitation; the emergency powers to be invoked; and the projected duration, which shall not exceed thirty days in the first instance. The Declaration shall be transmitted to the Assembly within six hours and shall lapse automatically if not ratified by a simple majority of the Assembly within seventy-two hours. Each renewal beyond the initial thirty days requires a fresh Assembly vote by absolute majority. No state of emergency may remain in force for more than one cumulative year without a mandatory referendum approved by sixty percent of participating citizens.
§ 18.
Automatic Sunset
Every emergency measure shall carry on its face a specified expiry date no later than the end of the declared emergency period. Emergency measures expire automatically on their stated date without any further action required. No emergency measure may be renewed more than three times. An emergency measure that has expired may not be reissued under a new emergency declaration unless a fresh genuine emergency independently justifies it. The Guardian Commission shall maintain a public Emergency Measures Register listing every active measure, its scope, its legal basis, its issuing body, and its expiry date, updated within twenty-four hours of any change.
§ 19.
Powers Prohibited During Any Emergency
Regardless of the nature, severity, or duration of any declared emergency, the following actions are absolutely prohibited: (a) dissolving, suspending, or preventing the Assembly from convening; (b) postponing or cancelling any scheduled lottery selection for any constitutional body; (c) dismissing, arresting, or preventing any member of the Guardian Commission, Constitutional Enforcement Directorate, or Public Auditor from performing their duties; (d) amending, suspending, or replacing this Constitution or any of its provisions; (e) extending the Council's own term of service or delaying membership rotation; (f) directing the Defense Forces against citizens of the Republic; (g) assuming control of the media, the Official Gazette, or public information systems; and (h) issuing emergency measures that advantage the Council's own position relative to the Assembly or the Courts. Any Council action falling within these prohibitions is void, constitutes Constitutional Subversion, and triggers automatic activation of Article XV.
§ 20.
Rights That May Be Temporarily and Narrowly Limited
During a validly declared and Assembly-ratified emergency, and strictly within the affected geographic area, the following rights may be temporarily limited to the minimum extent demonstrably necessary: freedom of movement; the right of public assembly; and specified economic activities where public safety requires restriction. Every limitation must be proportionate to the specific threat; confined to the affected area; published in the Official Gazette within twenty-four hours; reviewable by a Constitutional Jury within seven days of any citizen challenge; and automatically lifted the moment the specific condition justifying it ceases to exist.
§ 21.
Rights That Can Never Be Suspended
The following rights are absolute, non-derogable, and beyond the reach of any emergency declaration, constitutional amendment, or act of any body whatsoever: the right to life; freedom from torture, cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment; freedom from slavery and forced labour; the right to recognition as a person before the law; freedom of thought, conscience, and religion in their internal dimension; the right of habeas corpus; the right to a fair hearing before a Citizen Jury; the prohibition on retroactive criminal laws; the prohibition on collective punishment; all rights of children; the rights of minorities and indigenous peoples; and the Civic Oath and its protections. No generation, no majority, no crisis, and no emergency grants authority to suspend these rights.
§ 22.
Post-Emergency Accountability
Within sixty days of the termination of any state of emergency, the Council shall present to the Assembly a full written account of every emergency measure issued; the factual basis for each; the rights and freedoms affected; the duration and geographic scope; and the outcome achieved. The Assembly shall determine by simple majority whether each measure was justified, proportionate, and lawfully issued. Unjustified measures shall be formally censured in the public record. Any Council member found to have issued an emergency measure in bad faith or for political advantage shall be referred for impeachment proceedings.

Article XV
📜
Supremacy, Ratification & Founding
Legal Authority & Ratification
§ 1.
Supremacy Clause
This Constitution is the supreme law of the Grand Equestrian Republic. Every law, decree, judicial order, executive directive, military command, civic act, and official decision within the territory and jurisdiction of the Republic derives its authority from, and must remain consistent with, this Constitution. Any act of government inconsistent with this Constitution is void from the moment of its commission, regardless of who performed it, what office they held, or what authority they claimed.
§ 2.
Source of Constitutional Authority
The authority of this Constitution derives solely and entirely from the free, informed, and deliberate consent of the citizens of the Grand Equestrian Republic. No monarch, no military, no religious institution, no foreign power, no hereditary lineage, no political party, and no single individual confers or may confer legitimacy upon this Constitution. It stands because the people have chosen it, and it endures only so long as the people, through the processes prescribed herein, maintain it. This principle is the foundation upon which every article, every institution, and every civic act of this Republic rests.
§ 3.
Ratification Process
This Constitution shall acquire full legal force upon completion of the following ratification process: (a) Founding Convention — a Founding Convention of one thousand and one citizens, selected by lot from a preliminary Civic Registry, shall convene to deliberate upon this Constitution for a period of no fewer than ninety days. The Convention shall have full power to propose amendments, which shall be incorporated if approved by two-thirds of Convention members; (b) Public Review Period — following the Convention, the final text shall be published in full and made freely available to every citizen for a period of sixty days, during which any citizen may submit formal commentary to the Convention record; (c) Founding Referendum — the Constitution shall be put to a direct vote of all registered citizens. It shall be ratified upon approval by sixty percent of participating citizens, provided that no fewer than fifty percent of all registered citizens participate. Upon ratification, the result shall be recorded in the Founding Act of the Republic, which shall be preserved in perpetuity as the original instrument of the Republic's legitimacy.
§ 4.
The Institutional Supremacy Ladder
Where two or more institutions of the Republic come into direct and irreconcilable conflict, the following order of constitutional supremacy shall determine resolution: (1) This Constitution — supreme above all; (2) Constitutional Jury rulings — binding interpretations of this Constitution in specific disputes; (3) The Guardian Commission — as custodian of constitutional integrity, its findings on procedural and registry matters are binding on all bodies; (4) The Assembly — as the primary legislative authority, its laws bind the Council, Courts, and all commissions; (5) The Courts — whose judgments bind all parties before them; (6) The Council — whose executive directives bind ministries and agencies; (7) All other bodies — commissions, offices, and local assemblies, in order of their jurisdictional scope. No body may act contrary to a higher authority in this ladder without a Constitutional Jury ruling authorizing the departure.
§ 5.
Constitutional Interpretation
This Constitution shall be interpreted in accordance with the following principles, applied in order: (a) Text — the plain and ordinary meaning of the words as understood at the time of ratification; (b) Purpose — the democratic and Aristotelian principles that animate the provision in question, as recorded in the Convention deliberations; (c) Consistency — the interpretation that best preserves the coherence and internal logic of the Constitution as a whole; and (d) Liberty — where genuine ambiguity remains, the interpretation that most expands the rights and civic participation of citizens shall be preferred over the interpretation that contracts them. No institution may claim an exclusive or permanent right to interpret this Constitution; all authoritative interpretations are made by Constitutional Juries as disputes arise, informed by these principles.
The Founding Protocol
§ 6.
Year Zero — The First Three Hundred and Sixty-Five Days
Upon ratification, the Republic shall enter a Founding Year during which the constitutional order is established in the following mandatory sequence: Days 1–30 — the Founding Convention, having completed ratification, shall appoint a Caretaker Council of twenty-one citizens by lot to exercise minimum necessary executive functions pending establishment of permanent bodies. The Caretaker Council shall have no legislative power and may not make binding policy decisions beyond day-to-day administration; Days 1–60 — the Guardian Commission shall be constituted by lot from the preliminary Civic Registry and shall immediately begin compiling the full Civic Registry; Days 61–120 — the Guardian Commission shall conduct the first civic lottery to seat the Assembly of three thousand citizens; Days 121–150 — the Assembly, upon seating, shall take its Civic Oaths, elect its first Committee Chairs by lot, and conduct its first session; Days 151–180 — the Assembly shall pass the Founding Budget, providing guaranteed funding for all constitutional bodies for the first full year; Days 181–210 — the Guardian Commission shall conduct the lottery for the Council of five hundred citizens; Days 211–240 — the Council shall be seated, sworn, and assume executive functions, at which point the Caretaker Council is dissolved; Days 241–300 — the Office of the Public Auditor, the Referendum Commission, and all other constitutional bodies shall be constituted by lot; Days 301–365 — the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate shall be established and its first officers sworn.
§ 7.
Guaranteed Founding Budget
The Founding Convention shall, before ratification, establish a Founding Fund drawn from the general revenues of the transitional state, sufficient to fund all constitutional bodies and their operations for no fewer than two full years. The Founding Fund shall be held in trust by the Guardian Commission and disbursed only upon formal requisition by the relevant body. No body of the transitional state, no creditor, and no foreign entity may attach, freeze, redirect, or in any way encumber the Founding Fund. The Founding Fund is the financial foundation of the Republic's independence and shall be treated as inviolable from the moment of its establishment.
§ 8.
Transitional Legitimacy
During the Founding Year, all acts of the Caretaker Council and the Guardian Commission that are consistent with this Constitution shall have full legal force. Citizens, institutions, and international partners may rely upon these acts as lawful exercises of authority under this Constitution. The Caretaker Council shall publish a full record of every decision taken during the Founding Year, which shall be reviewed by the seated Assembly within thirty days of its first session. Any act of the Caretaker Council found inconsistent with this Constitution by the Assembly may be annulled by simple majority vote.

Article XVI
⚔
The Constitutional Enforcement Directorate
A constitution without enforcement is a prayer. The Directorate is the sword behind the law — loyal not to any person, office, or faction, but solely to this Constitution and the citizens it protects.
Establishment & Independence
§ 1.
Establishment
There is hereby established the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate — an independent, armed civic body whose sole mandate is the enforcement of this Constitution and the orders of the Courts, the Guardian Commission, and the Constitutional Juries of the Republic. The Directorate is not a police force, a military unit, or an instrument of any branch of government. It is an instrument of the Constitution itself, answerable to no single institution but to the constitutional order as a whole.
§ 2.
Composition & Selection
The Directorate shall be led by a Board of Directors of seven citizens selected by lot from the Civic Registry, serving staggered three-year terms. Board members must have no prior affiliation with the military, any law enforcement body, or any political organization within the preceding ten years. The operational officers of the Directorate shall be professional civil servants recruited through open, merit-based examination. No officer of the Directorate may be a member of any political party, ideological organization, or religious body that exercises institutional direction over its members. Every member of the Directorate — Board and officer alike — shall swear the Civic Oath and an additional Directorate Oath pledging loyalty exclusively to this Constitution.
§ 3.
Powers & Jurisdiction
The Directorate shall have the power and duty to: enforce all orders of Citizen Juries, Constitutional Juries, and the Courts, including by physical compulsion where lawfully authorized; arrest and detain any person — regardless of office, rank, or status — against whom a Citizen Jury has issued a warrant or removal order; enforce Cease and Desist Orders, Mandatory Orders, and contempt findings of the Courts; protect the physical security of the Guardian Commission, the Oath Registry, the Civic Registry, and all constitutional records; and respond to any attempt to seize, suspend, or destroy a constitutional institution. The Directorate may not be directed by the Assembly, the Council, or any Minister. It receives its orders exclusively from the Courts, the Guardian Commission, and Constitutional Juries.
§ 4.
Accountability
The Directorate shall publish a full public record of every enforcement action taken, every arrest made, and every order executed within seventy-two hours of the action. Its budget is constitutionally guaranteed and audited annually by the Office of the Public Auditor. Any officer of the Directorate who executes an unlawful order, uses disproportionate force, or acts beyond their constitutional mandate is personally criminally liable. The existence of a superior's order is not a defense. A standing Directorate Oversight Jury of twenty-one citizens, selected by lot annually, shall review all Directorate operations and may refer misconduct to the Courts.
§ 5.
Chain of Enforcement
When a constitutional order requires enforcement, the following chain shall apply: the issuing body — Court, Guardian Commission, or Constitutional Jury — shall transmit the order to the Court Registry; the Court Registry shall formally serve the order upon the subject and notify the Directorate; the subject shall have forty-eight hours to comply voluntarily; upon non-compliance, the Directorate shall be authorized to enforce the order physically, using the minimum force necessary; all enforcement actions shall be documented and published. No enforcement action may be initiated without a formally registered order. No verbal, informal, or unpublished order carries enforcement authority.

Article XVII
🛡
Defense of the Constitutional Order
This Republic shall not fall quietly. Every citizen, every institution, and every officer of this Republic bears a personal duty to resist the destruction of the democratic order — by any means this Constitution authorizes.
Anti-Coup Provisions
§ 1.
The Crime of Constitutional Subversion
Constitutional Subversion is hereby defined as the gravest crime against the Grand Equestrian Republic. It is committed by any person or body that: seizes or attempts to seize control of any constitutional institution by force, fraud, or coercion; suspends, dissolves, or attempts to suspend or dissolve this Constitution or any of its institutions without following the amendment process prescribed in Article IX; uses military force, armed threat, or mass coercion to direct or override the decisions of any constitutional body; or declares, establishes, or attempts to establish any form of government, authority, or rule inconsistent with this Constitution. Constitutional Subversion carries no statute of limitations and admits no defense of superior orders, necessity, or emergency. It is punishable by permanent removal from all civic life and the most severe custodial sentence the Sentencing Framework allows.
§ 2.
Duty to Resist
Every citizen of the Grand Equestrian Republic, and every officer of every constitutional institution, has a constitutional duty to resist Constitutional Subversion by all lawful means available to them. This duty is not passive — it is active. When a citizen or officer has credible knowledge of an attempt at Constitutional Subversion, they are obligated to: immediately report the attempt to the Guardian Commission and the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate; refuse to comply with any order, directive, or command issued by or in furtherance of the subversive act; and, where their position allows, take affirmative steps to protect the integrity of constitutional institutions. No person shall be punished, dismissed, demoted, or disadvantaged for fulfilling this duty. Any act of retaliation against a person exercising the Duty to Resist is itself an act of Constitutional Subversion.
§ 3.
Institutional Continuity
No act of Constitutional Subversion — however complete in appearance — shall extinguish the legal existence of this Constitution or the institutions it creates. If any institution is forcibly suspended or destroyed, it shall be deemed to continue in legal existence and shall be reconstituted as soon as conditions permit, with full authority retroactive to the date of its suspension. Any laws, orders, or decisions made by a subversive authority during a period of unconstitutional control are void ab initio — from the beginning — and shall have no legal force once the constitutional order is restored. The restored institutions shall not be bound by any obligation incurred by the subversive authority.
§ 4.
Constitutional Watchdog Network
The Guardian Commission shall maintain a Constitutional Watchdog Network — a distributed system of secure, redundant records of this Constitution, the Civic Registry, the Oath Registry, and all foundational constitutional documents — stored across multiple independent locations within and, under treaty, outside the Republic. The purpose of the Network is to ensure that no single act of destruction, seizure, or suppression can erase the constitutional record. In the event of an attack on constitutional institutions, the Network shall automatically activate a Constitutional Emergency Protocol, notifying all registered citizens of the threat and providing instructions for the reconstitution of constitutional bodies.
§ 5.
Military Non-Intervention Oath
Every commissioned officer of the Defense Forces shall, upon commissioning and upon each promotion, swear a specific Military Non-Intervention Oath in addition to the Civic Oath. This oath shall state: "I swear that I will never use the armed forces of the Grand Equestrian Republic against its constitutional institutions, its elected or selected bodies, or its citizens in the service of any unconstitutional authority. If ordered to do so, I will refuse, report the order to the Guardian Commission and the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate, and do everything within my lawful power to prevent such use of force. I understand that an order to commit Constitutional Subversion is not a lawful military order and carries no military obligation." Breach of this oath constitutes Constitutional Subversion and shall be prosecuted accordingly.
Institutional Self-Defense
§ 6.
Emergency Constitutional Session
If the Guardian Commission, the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate, or any ten members of the Assembly jointly determine that a credible, imminent threat to the constitutional order exists, they may convene an Emergency Constitutional Session of a Constitutional Jury of five hundred and one citizens within seventy-two hours. The Emergency Session shall have the power to: declare a Constitutional Emergency; authorize the Directorate to take protective action; suspend the civic duties of any officer credibly implicated in the threat pending investigation; and issue emergency orders binding on all institutions. An Emergency Constitutional Session may not suspend this Constitution, the rights guaranteed in Article II, or the Civic Oath. It exists solely to protect the constitutional order, not to govern in its place.
§ 7.
Automatic Sanctions
Upon a formal finding of Constitutional Subversion by a Constitutional Jury, the following sanctions shall apply automatically and immediately, without any further proceeding: all civic powers of the convicted person or body are suspended; all assets under their direct control in their civic capacity are frozen pending audit; all orders, laws, and decisions issued by them since the commencement of the subversive act are suspended pending Constitutional Jury review; and international treaty partners shall be notified through the diplomatic service. These automatic sanctions are not punishments — they are protective measures to prevent further harm to the constitutional order pending full legal proceedings.

Article XVIII
⚖
The Permanence of the Republic
Democracies do not die in a single blow. They are eroded — slowly, quietly, one compromise at a time. This Article exists to name that erosion, and to make it impossible to mistake for progress.
§ 1.
The Eternal Core
The following principles of this Constitution are declared permanent and irrevocable. They may not be amended, suspended, repealed, or abridged by any process whatsoever — not by referendum, not by Constitutional Jury, not by unanimous Assembly vote, not by any future constitutional convention, and not by any claimed emergency: the selection of civic officers by lot; the prohibition on elections for legislative and executive office; the prohibition on any singular head of state; the guarantee of fundamental rights in Article II; the Civic Oath and its explicit renunciations; the prohibition on political money; the independence of the Guardian Commission and the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate; and the principle that the authority of this Constitution derives from the consent of the citizens. These are the soul of the Republic. They are beyond the reach of any generation, any majority, and any crisis.
§ 2.
Democratic Erosion — Named and Prohibited
The Republic recognizes that constitutional democracies are most often destroyed not by sudden coups but by gradual erosion. The following acts are therefore named, defined, and prohibited as forms of democratic erosion, regardless of how they are framed or justified: the systematic weakening of independent institutions through budget reduction, personnel purges, or mandate restrictions without Constitutional Jury authorization; the use of emergency powers beyond their constitutional duration or scope; the concentration of appointment powers in a single body or person; the suppression, harassment, or intimidation of civic officers, Legal Guides, journalists, or members of the Guardian Commission; the manipulation of the Civic Registry to exclude or disadvantage any group of citizens; and any public campaign to delegitimize a constitutional institution on grounds not established by Constitutional Jury ruling. Each of these acts, if proven before a Constitutional Jury, constitutes an offence against the Republic.
§ 3.
The Living Constitution
This Constitution is a living document — capable of growth, adaptation, and refinement through the processes prescribed in Article IX. It is not a fixed monument but a shared commitment, renewed by each generation that chooses to live under it. The citizens of the Grand Equestrian Republic are its authors, its guardians, and its ultimate enforcement. No institution created by this Constitution is greater than the Constitution itself. No generation may bind all future generations to a particular reading of its provisions. And no crisis — however severe, however genuine — justifies the abandonment of the principles upon which this Republic was founded. The Constitution endures because the people choose, generation after generation, that it shall.

Article XIX
🗺
Territorial Organization & Devolved Governance
The Grand Equestrian Republic inherits the full sovereign territory of the former Equestrian Dominions. Canterlot, seat of the former Crown, is now the constitutional capital of the Republic — its authority no longer royal, but civic.
The Unitary Devolved Structure
§ 1.
Constitutional Capital
Canterlot shall serve as the constitutional capital and the seat of all national institutions of the Grand Equestrian Republic. The authority formerly vested in the Equestrian Crown by virtue of Canterlot's status is hereby transferred in its entirety to the constitutional order established by this document. Canterlot retains ultimate legislative sovereignty on behalf of the Republic — not as a royal prerogative, but as the seat of the democratically constituted Assembly and Council. This sovereignty is held in trust for all citizens of the Republic equally and may not be wielded for the advantage of any territory, class, or people above another.
§ 2.
Nature of Devolved Authority
The Grand Equestrian Republic is a Devolved Unitary State. Ultimate constitutional sovereignty resides in Canterlot and is expressed through the institutions created by this Constitution. Authority is delegated — not surrendered — to the territories, autonomous regions, and special administrative zones recognized herein. All delegated authority flows from this Constitution; it may be expanded, modified, or — upon Constitutional Jury ruling — withdrawn if a territory acts in fundamental violation of the constitutional order. Devolution does not create separate sovereignties; it creates structured, constitutionally guaranteed spaces for self-governance within a unified Republic.
The Recognized Territories
§ 3.
Commonwealth New Mareland
New Mareland is recognized as a Commonwealth Territory of the Grand Equestrian Republic, retaining its cultural identity, internal governance structures, and territorial integrity under the Unification Treaty. New Mareland shall govern itself through its own Assembly, Council, and Courts constituted by lot on the same principles as the national bodies. The Commonwealth designation entitles New Mareland to full representation in the national Assembly proportional to its registered citizenry. National constitutional law applies in full; New Mareland may enact additional local legislation that does not conflict with this Constitution.
§ 4.
The Crystal Empire
The Crystal Empire is recognized as an Autonomous Region of the Grand Equestrian Republic, retaining its distinct cultural heritage, ceremonial traditions, and territorial identity. The Crystal Empire shall maintain its own governance institutions constituted by lot. The ceremonial role of the Crystal Throne, having been separated from political authority by the Unification Treaty, may be preserved as a cultural institution without civic power, subject to the Crystal Empire's own constitutional arrangements, provided these arrangements are consistent with this Constitution and do not confer any governmental authority upon the Throne or its occupant.
§ 5.
Puerto Caballo
Puerto Caballo is recognized as an Incorporated Territory of the Grand Equestrian Republic with full District status. Its citizens enjoy the complete rights and civic privileges of this Constitution. Puerto Caballo shall be organized into Neighborhoods and Districts under Article XI and shall participate in national civic lotteries on equal footing with all other territories. Its unique maritime heritage and cultural character shall be protected under Article I §10.
§ 6.
The Buffalo Chiefdom
The Buffalo Chiefdom is recognized as an Indigenous Autonomous Zone of the Grand Equestrian Republic. The Buffalo peoples shall retain full sovereignty over their ancestral lands, customary governance structures, and traditional practices. The Buffalo Chiefdom shall not be subject to civic lottery selection for its internal governance — its own traditional decision-making processes shall govern internal matters. However, the Buffalo Chiefdom shall be represented in the national Assembly by delegates chosen through a process of the Chiefdom's own design, and its citizens shall enjoy the full protection of Article I rights. No resource extraction, land use change, or infrastructure development may occur within Buffalo ancestral lands without the free, prior, and informed consent of the Buffalo Chiefdom's governing authority.
§ 7.
The United Dragon Lands
The United Dragon Lands are recognized as a Special Administrative Territory of the Grand Equestrian Republic, governed under a Special Accession Treaty negotiated between the Dragon Lords and the Republic. The United Dragon Lands retain their internal governance traditions and the authority of the Dragon Lord Council over internal matters. The Republic and the United Dragon Lands shall share jurisdiction over trade, environmental protection, and cross-border movement under the terms of the Special Accession Treaty. Citizens of the United Dragon Lands residing within the Republic's territories enjoy full constitutional rights. The Special Accession Treaty shall be ratified by the Assembly and may not be unilaterally altered by the Republic without the consent of the Dragon Lord Council.
§ 8.
Staliongrad
Staliongrad, formerly an independent communist republic, accedes to the Grand Equestrian Republic under the Unification Treaty as a Special Autonomous Region with enhanced devolved powers. Staliongrad retains its own constitutional arrangements for internal governance, provided these arrangements guarantee the fundamental rights enumerated in Article I of this Constitution. Staliongrad's accession shall not be construed as an endorsement or prohibition of any particular economic philosophy within its internal arrangements. National constitutional law supersedes Staliongrad's internal law in all matters touching upon: fundamental rights, citizenship, criminal law of national scope, defense, and foreign affairs. A Joint Coordination Commission, composed of representatives of both Staliongrad and the national Council, shall manage the interface between Staliongrad's autonomous arrangements and the national constitutional order.
§ 9.
The Unification Treaty
The Unification Treaty, signed under the authority of the former Triarchy and accepted by the governing authorities of each territory, is hereby recognized as a foundational instrument of the Grand Equestrian Republic alongside this Constitution. Where the Unification Treaty grants rights or protections to territories that exceed those specified in this Constitution, the Treaty provisions shall prevail. Where the Treaty conflicts with the fundamental rights guaranteed in Article I, this Constitution shall prevail. The Unification Treaty may not be amended, abrogated, or superseded without the consent of the relevant territory and a two-thirds vote of the national Assembly.

Article XX
🏛
Departments & Agencies of the Republic
The Ministries of the former Triarchy served the Crown. The Departments of the Grand Equestrian Republic serve the people. The name changes; the duty deepens.
The Departmental System
§ 1.
Transition from Ministries to Departments
All Ministries of the former Equestrian Triarchy are hereby dissolved as royal institutions and reconstituted as Departments of the Grand Equestrian Republic. The word "Ministry" and the title "Minister" in their monarchical sense are abolished from civic nomenclature. Each Department shall be headed by a Director-General appointed by the Council from a pool of qualified professionals recommended by independent expert panels, serving fixed administrative terms of three years. Directors-General are administrators, not politicians; they implement policy determined by the Assembly and the Council and are fully accountable to both.
§ 2.
Principal Departments of the Republic
The following principal Departments are hereby established, succeeding the corresponding Ministries of the former Triarchy: (a) Department of Defense — succeeding the Ministry of Defense, responsible for the national defense forces and military policy under civilian Council oversight; (b) Department of Foreign Affairs — responsible for diplomatic relations, international treaties, and the representation of the Republic abroad; (c) Department of Justice — responsible for the administration of the Courts, the Legal Guides corps, the Public Advocates Office, and the rule of law; (d) Department of the Interior — responsible for national law enforcement, civil order, territorial administration, and the National Police; (e) Department of the Treasury — responsible for public finance, revenue collection, and economic administration; (f) Department of Health & Social Welfare — responsible for public health, social services, and the welfare of citizens; (g) Department of Education & Culture — responsible for civic education, cultural heritage, and the arts; (h) Department of Infrastructure & Environment — responsible for public works, transportation, and environmental protection; (i) Department of Agriculture & Food Security — responsible for agricultural policy and food supply; and (j) Department of Science & Technology — responsible for research, innovation, and technological development. Additional Departments may be established by the Assembly through Ordinary Law.
Intelligence & Investigative Agencies
§ 3.
Bureau of Investigation of the Republic (BIR)
The Bureau of Investigation of the Republic (BIR) is hereby established as the successor to the former Royal Bureau of Investigation (RBI), with all institutional knowledge, personnel, and operational continuity preserved. The BIR is the Republic's principal civilian investigative agency responsible for: investigating serious crimes of national scope; combating organized crime, corruption, and crimes against the constitutional order; providing investigative support to District and national Courts; and maintaining the Republic's forensic and intelligence capabilities in the civilian domain. The BIR operates under the Department of Justice and is fully subject to judicial oversight. No investigation may be opened against any citizen without a warrant issued by a Citizen Jury upon demonstrated probable cause.
§ 4.
The Secret Military Intelligence League of Equestria (S.M.I.L.E.)
S.M.I.L.E. is hereby rechartered as a Special Intelligence Agency of the Grand Equestrian Republic, retaining its institutional name, operational identity, and accumulated expertise in the national interest. S.M.I.L.E. shall operate under the joint oversight of the Department of Defense and the Department of Foreign Affairs, with its budget subject to full audit by the Office of the Public Auditor under sealed review procedures protecting operational security. S.M.I.L.E.'s mandate encompasses foreign intelligence, counterintelligence, and threats to national security originating outside the Republic's borders. S.M.I.L.E. shall have no authority to conduct operations against citizens of the Republic within its borders except upon a sealed warrant issued by a Constitutional Jury. An annual sealed report on S.M.I.L.E.'s activities shall be presented to a Security Oversight Committee of the Assembly composed of fifteen members selected by lot with appropriate security clearance.
§ 5.
Agency Accountability
All Departments and Agencies of the Republic shall: submit annual public reports of their activities, expenditures, and performance to the Assembly; appear before Assembly Committees upon request; comply fully with audits by the Office of the Public Auditor; and maintain publicly accessible registers of their principal decisions and policies. No Department or Agency may claim exemption from constitutional oversight on grounds of operational sensitivity except as specifically authorized by Fundamental Law and subject to sealed review by a Constitutional Jury. The existence of any Department or Agency, its budget allocation, and its principal mandate shall always be public.

Article XXI
⚔
The Grand Equestrian Defense Forces (E.U.P.B.H.)
The defense of the Grand Equestrian Republic belongs to all its peoples — Earth Pony, Unicorn, Pegasus, Bat Pony, and Human alike. The E.U.P.B.H. is not the sword of a crown. It is the shield of a Republic.
Structure & Composition
§ 1.
Establishment of the E.U.P.B.H.
The defense and public order forces of the Grand Equestrian Republic are organized under the Grand Equestrian Defense Forces framework, designated the E.U.P.B.H. — representing the five peoples who constitute the Republic: Earth Ponies, Unicorns, Pegasi, Bat Ponies, and Humans. The E.U.P.B.H. designation reflects the Republic's commitment to the equal contribution and equal protection of all its peoples in the defense of their shared constitutional order. The E.U.P.B.H. supersedes and dissolves the former E.U.P. designation, which excluded Bat Ponies and Humans from formal recognition within the defense structure. All five peoples shall serve, lead, and be protected equally within every tier of the E.U.P.B.H.
§ 2.
Tier 1 — Community Police
Community Police shall be organized at the Neighborhood level under the authority of the Neighborhood Council and the Department of the Interior. Their mandate is the enforcement of local laws, the maintenance of community safety, and the service of their communities. Community Police are equipped with non-lethal implements and may employ race-based abilities within the bounds of proportionate force as defined by Fundamental Law. They shall be recruited from and accountable to the communities they serve. No Community Police officer may enforce national law without referral to Tier 2.
§ 3.
Tier 2 — National Police
The National Police shall enforce national law, maintain public order, and respond to crimes and emergencies across the Republic's territory. They operate under the Department of the Interior and are subject to full civilian judicial oversight. National Police are authorized to carry standard sidearms as prescribed by Fundamental Law. The National Police shall include specialized units — including a Special Weapons and Tactics (SWAT) division — to address high-threat situations including bomb threats, active threats to life, and situations requiring tactical response. The use of SWAT units against lawful demonstrations or peaceful assemblies is prohibited and constitutes an abuse of force subject to criminal prosecution.
§ 4.
Tier 3 — National Guard
The National Guard shall handle nationwide emergencies and maintain civil order at the District level during declared emergencies. National Guard members shall be drawn in part from Tier 5 military personnel and in part from civilian volunteers. The National Guard operates under the joint authority of the Council and the relevant District Council during declared emergencies. Militia formations that formerly operated under warlord command are hereby dissolved as constitutional entities; any voluntary defense organization operating within the Republic must be registered with, and subject to the oversight of, the Department of Defense. No armed formation may operate under private command independent of the constitutional chain of authority.
§ 5.
Tier 4 — Constitutional Guard
The former Royal Guard is hereby reconstituted as the Constitutional Guard of the Grand Equestrian Republic. The Constitutional Guard's mandate is transformed from the protection of the Royal Triarchy to the protection of the Republic's constitutional institutions — including the Assembly, the Council, the Guardian Commission, the Office of the Public Auditor, and the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate. The Constitutional Guard shall manage crowd control and respond to threats to constitutional institutions. It may participate in foreign operations under direct orders from the Council authorized by the Assembly. The Constitutional Guard answers to the Council collectively and may not be directed by any single officer, minister, or official unilaterally.
§ 6.
Tier 5 — Military
The Military constitutes the highest tier of the E.U.P.B.H. and is responsible for the national defense of the Grand Equestrian Republic against external threats. The Military is the most elite and best-equipped tier of the defense structure. It enforces martial law nationwide only upon formal declaration by the Assembly under Article XI §19 and §20. All provisions of Article X (The Defense Forces of the Republic) apply in full to the Military as Tier 5 of the E.U.P.B.H. The Military swears its oath to this Constitution, not to any person, office, or flag of non-democratic authority.
§ 7.
Race-Based Abilities — Regulation & Equality
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes that Earth Ponies, Unicorns, Pegasi, Bat Ponies, and Humans each possess distinct natural abilities relevant to defense and public order. The use of race-based abilities within the E.U.P.B.H. shall be: governed by Fundamental Law establishing standards of proportionality and prohibition on discriminatory application; equal in dignity across all races — no race's abilities shall be treated as superior or inferior in the eyes of the law; regulated to prevent the weaponization of race-based abilities against civilians in unauthorized circumstances; and developed through shared training programs that build mutual understanding among all five peoples of the E.U.P.B.H.

Article XXII
📜
The Civic Mandate — The People's Last Resort
When institutions fail, the people do not fall. The Civic Mandate is the constitutional recognition that sovereignty never leaves the hands of the citizens — not even when their government forgets it.
Foundation
§ 1.
The Mandate Declared
The Civic Mandate — successor to the Equestrian Ponies Mandate of the former constitutional order — is hereby declared a permanent constitutional safeguard of the Grand Equestrian Republic. It recognizes that when both the Assembly and the Council simultaneously fail to represent the interests of the citizens, act in corrupt concert against the public good, or are rendered ineffective by constitutional subversion, the citizens of the Republic retain an active, lawful, and constitutionally guaranteed right to restore governance through peaceful civic action. The Civic Mandate is a revolution without violence, fire, or bloodshed — a structured, constitutional pathway for the people to reclaim what belongs to them.
§ 2.
Conditions for Activation
The Civic Mandate may be activated when any two of the following conditions are simultaneously and demonstrably present: (a) the Assembly has failed to pass a budget, a required law, or a constitutionally mandated act for more than one hundred and eighty consecutive days without legitimate cause; (b) the Council has demonstrably acted in its own interest or in the interest of a faction rather than the common good, as evidenced by a finding of the Public Auditor or the Guardian Commission; (c) both the Assembly and the Council have refused to respond to a formally submitted citizen petition signed by ten percent of the registered citizenry within sixty days; (d) a Constitutional Jury has found that both branches have engaged in coordinated constitutional violations; or (e) the Guardian Commission formally certifies that the constitutional order is at imminent risk of systemic failure. Activation of the Civic Mandate is certified by the Guardian Commission.
Mechanisms of the Civic Mandate
§ 3.
Vote of No Confidence
Upon activation of the Civic Mandate, citizens may initiate a Vote of No Confidence in the Council, the Assembly, or both simultaneously, by submitting a petition bearing the verified signatures of fifteen percent of the registered citizenry to the Guardian Commission. The Guardian Commission shall administer the Vote of No Confidence within thirty days. A successful Vote of No Confidence — approved by a simple majority of participating citizens with a minimum turnout of forty percent — shall trigger the immediate dissolution of the implicated body and a new civic lottery selection within sixty days. During the transition, the Guardian Commission shall assume minimum necessary caretaker functions.
§ 4.
Harmony Assemblies
Harmony Assemblies are extraordinary civic gatherings of citizens convened under the Civic Mandate to deliberate on the failures of governance, propose reforms, and issue binding civic directives. A Harmony Assembly shall be convened by the Guardian Commission upon petition of ten percent of the registered citizenry. Each Harmony Assembly shall consist of one thousand and one citizens selected by lot from the full Civic Registry, representative of the demographic diversity of the Republic. A Harmony Assembly shall have the power to: issue a Declaration of Civic Concern publicly naming the failures of governance and demanding specific remedies; propose emergency constitutional amendments subject to subsequent referendum; recommend the dissolution of any civic body; and, with a two-thirds vote, issue a binding Civic Directive requiring the implicated institutions to take specified action within thirty days.
§ 5.
Harmony Clause Representatives
In the event that both the Assembly and the Council are dissolved under the Civic Mandate, the Republic shall not be left without governance. The Guardian Commission shall within fourteen days select by lot one hundred and one Harmony Clause Representatives from the Civic Registry to constitute a Transitional Civic Council. The Transitional Civic Council shall exercise minimum necessary executive and legislative functions — strictly limited to those required to maintain public services, protect rights, and prepare for the restoration of permanent constitutional bodies — for a period not exceeding one hundred and eighty days. The Transitional Civic Council shall have no power to pass Fundamental Laws, amend the Constitution, or extend its own tenure.
§ 6.
Civic Reforms Under the Mandate
Reforms proposed through the Civic Mandate process — whether by Harmony Assembly or Vote of No Confidence — shall be implemented through the following pathway: the proposed reform is published in the Official Gazette and open to public commentary for thirty days; the Guardian Commission assesses the reform for constitutional compatibility; a referendum is held within sixty days; and upon approval by sixty percent of participating citizens with a minimum turnout of forty percent, the reform is enacted as a Civic Mandate Law with the same force as an Assembly Fundamental Law. Civic Mandate Laws may not be repealed by the Assembly alone — their repeal requires a fresh referendum with the same threshold.
§ 7.
Limits of the Civic Mandate
The Civic Mandate is a constitutional safeguard, not a tool of faction or passion. The following actions may never be taken under the Civic Mandate: the suspension or abrogation of any right guaranteed in Article I; the targeting of any individual, community, or minority group for disadvantage; the installation of any person or body to permanent authority without a subsequent lottery or referendum; the suspension of this Constitution itself; or any action resembling or producing any of the non-democratic systems prohibited in Article I §3. The Guardian Commission shall refuse to certify any Civic Mandate action that violates these limits, and any attempt to use the Civic Mandate for non-constitutional ends shall be treated as Constitutional Subversion under Article XV.
Legitimacy Safeguards
§ 8.
Single Legitimacy Authority
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes that the most dangerous moment in any civic mandate process is when multiple actors simultaneously claim constitutional legitimacy. This Constitution therefore establishes a single, unambiguous legitimacy arbiter for all Civic Mandate disputes: the Guardian Commission. No group, faction, organization, movement, or self-declared assembly may invoke the Civic Mandate without Guardian Commission certification of activation conditions under §2. Any body claiming to exercise Civic Mandate powers without valid Guardian Commission certification is acting without constitutional authority, and its claimed acts are void. Where the Guardian Commission's own legitimacy is contested during a Civic Mandate process, the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate shall protect the Commission's physical and operational integrity pending resolution by a Constitutional Jury convened within seventy-two hours. There is no constitutional space for parallel legitimacy systems — the Civic Mandate is a single, unified, constitutionally governed process or it is not the Civic Mandate.
§ 9.
Multi-Institution Validation Requirement
No Civic Mandate activation may proceed on the certification of the Guardian Commission alone. Before any Civic Mandate mechanism — Vote of No Confidence, Harmony Assembly, or Harmony Clause Representatives — becomes operative, the activation must be independently validated by all three of the following: (a) The Constitutional Enforcement Directorate, which shall confirm that the conditions certified by the Guardian Commission are consistent with the factual record available to it; (b) A Constitutional Jury of one hundred and one citizens, convened within fourteen days of Guardian Commission certification, which shall conduct an evidentiary hearing and confirm by two-thirds vote that at least two activation conditions of §2 are demonstrably satisfied; and (c) Regional validation — the conditions must be independently verified as present by the District Emergency Councils of no fewer than one third of all Districts in the Republic. If any of these three validation requirements is not satisfied within thirty days of Guardian Commission certification, the activation lapses and a fresh certification process must begin from the beginning.
§ 10.
Evidentiary Standard for Activation
The activation conditions of §2 shall be assessed against a clear and convincing evidentiary standard — the same standard applied in constitutional court proceedings. Vague public dissatisfaction, declining approval ratings, political disagreement, or ideological opposition to the policies of the Assembly or Council do not satisfy any activation condition. Each condition requires documented, verifiable, institutional evidence: official findings of the Public Auditor or Guardian Commission; Constitutional Jury rulings; records of formally submitted and formally refused petitions; or documented failure of specific constitutional obligations over specific measurable timeframes. The Constitutional Jury conducting the validation hearing under §9 shall apply this evidentiary standard strictly and shall publish its full reasoning in the Official Gazette within seven days of its determination.
Anti-Capture & Anti-Manipulation Safeguards
§ 11.
Anti-Mobilized Minority Capture
The Civic Mandate must represent the broad sovereign will of the citizenry — not the organized energy of a motivated minority. The following safeguards prevent minority capture of the Civic Mandate process: petition signatures for activation conditions must be drawn from a genuinely representative cross-section of the citizenry, with no more than twenty percent of required signatures coming from any single District, territory, or demographic group; the Civic Information Integrity Commission shall audit all petition drives for signs of coordinated organizational domination and shall publish its findings before any Guardian Commission certification proceeds; and the Constitutional Jury conducting the validation hearing under §9 shall specifically assess whether the petition represents genuine broad civic concern or organized factional activism, and shall refuse to validate an activation that is demonstrably driven by a narrow organized movement rather than widespread authentic civic distress. The Civic Mandate is the people's tool — every safeguard in this section exists to keep it that way.
§ 12.
Information Integrity Verification
Before the Guardian Commission may certify a Civic Mandate activation, the Civic Information Integrity Commission shall conduct a mandatory Information Integrity Assessment of the information environment surrounding the claimed activation conditions. The Assessment shall determine whether: the public perception of institutional failure is consistent with documented factual evidence or has been materially shaped by disinformation, AI-generated propaganda, coordinated narrative manipulation, or foreign influence operations; the petition signatures were obtained through authentic civic concern or through organized psychological manipulation campaigns; and the claimed activation conditions are genuinely present in the constitutional record or are artifacts of manufactured outrage. A finding of material information manipulation shall suspend the certification process pending a clean information environment, and the manipulation itself shall be referred for prosecution as an offence against the democratic order under Articles XXVI and XXXIII. No Civic Mandate shall be activated on the basis of manufactured crisis.
§ 13.
Deliberation Cooling Period
The Civic Mandate is a constitutional instrument of last resort, not a mechanism of immediate emotional response. Between the filing of a petition seeking Civic Mandate activation and the Guardian Commission's certification assessment, a mandatory Deliberation Cooling Period of sixty days shall apply. During the Cooling Period: the petition shall be published in full in the Official Gazette; the implicated institutions shall have the opportunity to respond to the claimed activation conditions and to take remedial action that may resolve them; the Civic Information Integrity Commission shall conduct its Information Integrity Assessment; and citizens shall have the opportunity to submit formal commentary on whether the activation conditions are genuinely present. If the implicated institutions take verified remedial action that resolves the documented activation conditions during the Cooling Period, the certification process shall lapse and the institutions shall publish a Remediation Report within thirty days explaining the corrective actions taken. This mechanism gives institutions a constitutional opportunity to self-correct before the Mandate is invoked.
Duration, Recursion & Resolution
§ 14.
Time Limits & Automatic Expiry
All Civic Mandate powers are strictly time-limited. No Civic Mandate activation may remain operative for more than one hundred and eighty days in total. All mechanisms invoked under an activation — Votes of No Confidence, Harmony Assemblies, Harmony Clause Representative governance — expire automatically at the end of the one-hundred-and-eighty-day period unless the specific mechanism has already concluded its function. No activation may be immediately renewed upon expiry; a minimum period of one year must elapse before a fresh Civic Mandate petition may be filed. During that year, the restored or newly constituted institutions shall be given the institutional calm necessary to function without perpetual constitutional agitation. Repeated, overlapping, or chained Civic Mandate activations — where one activation immediately follows another in a pattern designed to maintain permanent constitutional pressure — are prohibited and shall be treated as an abuse of the Civic Mandate constituting Constitutional Subversion under Article XV.
§ 15.
Recursive Legitimacy Resolution
Where a recursive legitimacy crisis arises — a situation in which institutions reject a Civic Mandate claim while Civic Mandate actors reject institutional rulings, creating a cycle of mutually contested legitimacy — the following resolution protocol shall apply without exception: the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate shall immediately protect the physical integrity and operational continuity of the Guardian Commission as the single legitimacy arbiter; the Guardian Commission shall within forty-eight hours issue a Legitimacy Determination identifying which chain of constitutional authority is consistent with this Constitution's Legitimacy Supremacy Ladder under Article XIII §4; the Legitimacy Determination shall be immediately binding on all parties pending Constitutional Jury review; a Constitutional Jury of five hundred and one citizens shall be convened within seven days to conduct a definitive Legitimacy Ruling; and all parties — whether institutional or civic — must accept and comply with the Constitutional Jury's Legitimacy Ruling or be treated as engaging in Constitutional Subversion. There is no constitutional vacuum in the Grand Equestrian Republic. Legitimacy questions always have a constitutional answer, and that answer is always reached through process, not through power.
§ 16.
Constitutional Objective Standard
The Civic Mandate exists to defend the constitutional order — not to advance any ideology, political preference, cultural vision, economic philosophy, or factional interest. This distinction is fundamental and shall be strictly enforced. A Civic Mandate action that defends constitutional procedure, restores institutional accountability, or removes a demonstrated corrupt authority is constitutionally legitimate regardless of its political valence. A Civic Mandate action that seeks to impose a particular ideological, religious, economic, or cultural outcome — even if genuinely popular — is not constitutionally legitimate and shall not receive Guardian Commission certification. The Constitutional Jury conducting the validation hearing under §9 shall specifically assess whether the proposed Civic Mandate action is directed at restoring the constitutional order or at achieving a substantive political outcome, and shall refuse validation of the latter. The Civic Mandate is the Republic's immune system — it fights infection, it does not choose the Republic's values. Those are chosen through the ordinary processes of democratic deliberation.

Whistleblower Protection & the Truth Over Allegiance Doctrine
Truth, spoken in good faith against power, is an act of service to the Republic — regardless of who speaks it or where they come from. This Republic protects those who choose truth over allegiance.
Domestic Whistleblower Protections
§ 1.
The Right to Disclose
Every citizen and resident of the Grand Equestrian Republic who, in good faith, discloses information revealing: a violation of this Constitution or any law of the Republic; a serious threat to public health, safety, or the environment; the misuse of public funds or public authority; an act of corruption, fraud, or abuse of office; or a threat to the democratic constitutional order — shall be entitled to full protection under this Article. The right to disclose is a civic right of the highest order. No whistleblower shall suffer dismissal, demotion, prosecution, harassment, surveillance, or any other retaliation for a disclosure made in good faith.
§ 2.
The Whistleblower Tribunal
There is hereby established the Whistleblower Tribunal — an independent body of three citizens selected by lot from the Civic Registry, serving renewable one-year terms, supported by a permanent professional secretariat. The Tribunal shall: receive, assess, and protect disclosures; determine whether a disclosure qualifies for protection under this Article; investigate alleged retaliation and refer cases to the Courts; recommend remedies including reinstatement, compensation, and criminal referral; and publish annual reports on whistleblowing trends and the Republic's disclosure culture. The Tribunal operates independently of all Departments, Agencies, the Assembly, and the Council.
§ 3.
Retaliation as a Criminal Offence
Any person, Department, Agency, or civic body that retaliates against a whistleblower — including through termination, prosecution, surveillance, defamation, threats, or obstruction of the whistleblower's civic participation — commits the criminal offence of Whistleblower Retaliation. This offence shall be prosecuted before a Citizen Jury of thirty-one persons. Upon conviction, penalties shall include: personal criminal liability for the individual who ordered or carried out the retaliation; institutional sanctions against the Department or body involved; and full remediation of all harm suffered by the whistleblower including reinstatement, back compensation, and damages.
The Truth Over Allegiance Doctrine — The Foreign Spy Clause
§ 4.
The Foreign Spy Clause
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes that foreign operatives — agents, informants, or intelligence officers acting under the authority of a foreign government or organization — may possess knowledge of serious wrongdoing, imminent threats to the Republic, violations of Harmony, crimes against citizens of the Republic or its territories, or acts that, if disclosed, would serve the fundamental interests of justice and the constitutional order. This Constitution hereby establishes the Truth Over Allegiance Doctrine: a foreign operative who discloses such information to the Whistleblower Tribunal or to S.M.I.L.E. in good faith, without committing treasonous acts against the Republic in the course of the disclosure, shall be entitled to conditional protection under this Article, provided the conditions of §5 are satisfied.
§ 5.
Conditions for Foreign Operative Protection
Conditional protection under the Truth Over Allegiance Doctrine shall be granted only where the Whistleblower Tribunal, following a full assessment, finds that: (a) the disclosed information reveals a genuine serious wrongdoing, imminent threat, or violation of Harmony of material significance to the Republic or its citizens; (b) the disclosure was made voluntarily and in good faith, without coercion or deception of the Republic; (c) the foreign operative has not, in connection with the disclosure or otherwise, committed any act of treason against the Grand Equestrian Republic, including espionage directed at harming the Republic, sabotage of constitutional institutions, or the commission of violence against citizens; (d) the disclosure does not itself constitute or enable an act harmful to the Republic's constitutional order or the safety of its citizens; and (e) the foreign operative cooperates fully and honestly with the Tribunal's assessment process.
§ 6.
Truth Verification
The Whistleblower Tribunal may, in assessing a foreign operative's disclosure, employ any truth-verification method recognized by Fundamental Law as reliable and ethically permissible — including, where applicable and consented to by the operative, magical truth-verification methods available within the Republic. No truth-verification method may be applied without the informed consent of the operative. Evidence obtained through unconsented truth-verification shall be inadmissible in any proceeding. The results of truth-verification are advisory to the Tribunal; they do not automatically determine the outcome of the Tribunal's assessment.
§ 7.
The Citizenship Through Truth Programme (CTTP)
A foreign operative who has received protection under the Truth Over Allegiance Doctrine and whose disclosure has materially served the constitutional order, the safety of citizens, or the cause of justice in the Grand Equestrian Republic may apply to the Citizenship Through Truth Programme. The CTTP provides a pathway to permanent residency and, after a period of no fewer than three years of lawful residency and demonstrated civic participation, to full citizenship of the Grand Equestrian Republic. The CTTP shall be administered by the Department of Foreign Affairs in conjunction with the Whistleblower Tribunal. Citizenship through the CTTP confers all rights of citizenship including eligibility for the Civic Registry. The CTTP pathway shall be established and its conditions defined in detail by Fundamental Law enacted by the Assembly.

Constitutional Rapid Action Framework
A democracy that cannot act quickly enough to survive is not a democracy — it is a ruin. The Rapid Action Framework exists so that speed and liberty are never forced to choose between each other.
Emergency Mobilization
§ 1.
The Rapid Action Council
Upon the declaration of a national emergency under Article XI, the Council shall immediately constitute within itself a Rapid Action Council — a standing sub-body of twenty-five Council members selected by internal lot — empowered to act with the speed and decisiveness required by the emergency without convening the full Council of five hundred. The Rapid Action Council shall have all the executive powers of the full Council during the emergency period, subject to the prohibitions of Article XI §19. Decisions of the Rapid Action Council require a simple majority of its twenty-five members and take effect immediately. All Rapid Action Council decisions shall be transmitted to the full Council within six hours and shall be reviewed by the full Council within forty-eight hours; the full Council may override any Rapid Action Council decision by absolute majority.
§ 2.
Emergency Budget Authorization
Upon declaration of a national emergency, an Emergency Budget Reserve — constitutionally pre-funded at no less than five percent of the prior year's national budget, held in trust by the Office of the Public Auditor — shall be immediately available to the Rapid Action Council without prior Assembly approval. Emergency Budget expenditures shall be reported to the Assembly within seventy-two hours and audited by the Public Auditor within thirty days of the emergency's conclusion. No Emergency Budget funds may be used for any purpose unrelated to the declared emergency. Any misapplication of Emergency Budget funds constitutes civic corruption under Article IX.
§ 3.
Emergency Legislative Fast-Track
During a declared national emergency, the Assembly may invoke the Emergency Legislative Fast-Track, reducing the mandatory deliberation period for Ordinary Laws to forty-eight hours and for Fundamental Laws to seven days. Fast-Track legislation requires the same voting thresholds as standard legislation but may be passed in emergency session with a reduced quorum of one thousand members. All Fast-Track legislation carries an automatic sunset clause of one hundred and eighty days and must be reconsidered by the full Assembly under standard procedures within that period or it lapses. Fast-Track legislation may not amend this Constitution, alter the Sentencing Framework retroactively, or expand the emergency powers of the Council beyond those defined in Article XI.
§ 4.
Unified Command Protocol
During a declared national emergency involving active military threat, natural catastrophe, or infrastructure failure, the Rapid Action Council shall designate a Crisis Coordinator — a senior civil servant from the relevant Department — to serve as the single operational point of coordination across all Departments and Agencies. The Crisis Coordinator has no political authority; they coordinate logistics, information, and resource deployment only. All Departments and Agencies are obligated to share information with the Crisis Coordinator in real time. The Crisis Coordinator answers to the Rapid Action Council and ceases to function the moment the emergency is lifted.
§ 5.
Automatic Judicial Oversight During Emergency
Upon invocation of the Rapid Action Framework, a standing Emergency Review Jury of fifteen citizens shall be constituted by lot within twenty-four hours by the Guardian Commission. The Emergency Review Jury shall sit continuously throughout the emergency period and shall have power to review any Rapid Action Council decision, any Fast-Track law, or any emergency measure upon petition by any citizen. The Emergency Review Jury may suspend any measure found to exceed constitutional bounds within forty-eight hours of petition. Its decisions are binding immediately. The Emergency Review Jury dissolves automatically upon termination of the emergency and its full record is published within seven days.

Article XXVI
📚
Constitutional Precedent & Interpretive Doctrine
Law without consistency is not law — it is chance. The precedent doctrine exists so that the constitutional rights of a citizen in the North mean the same thing as the constitutional rights of a citizen in the South.
Precedent System
§ 1.
The Doctrine of Precedent
Constitutional Jury rulings on questions of constitutional law shall constitute binding precedent upon all lower Courts and all subsequent Citizen Juries considering the same constitutional question. Ordinary Citizen Jury verdicts on questions of fact do not constitute binding precedent; each case of fact is decided on its own merits. The distinction between constitutional questions — which create precedent — and questions of fact — which do not — shall be determined by the Legal Guides panel of the Court Registry before trial commences. This doctrine ensures that constitutional meaning is consistent and predictable across the Republic while preserving the jury's role in determining the facts of each individual case.
§ 2.
The Constitutional Precedent Registry
The Court Registry shall maintain a publicly accessible Constitutional Precedent Registry — a permanent, searchable record of all Constitutional Jury rulings on questions of constitutional law, organized by article, section, and subject matter. Every entry shall include: the full text of the ruling; the constitutional question addressed; the facts of the case in summary; the reasoning of the jury as recorded by the Legal Guide; and the date and composition of the jury. Any citizen, Legal Guide, or Court officer may search the Registry freely. The Registry shall be published online and in hard copy at every Court Registry location throughout the Republic.
§ 3.
Departing from Precedent
A Constitutional Jury may depart from an existing precedent only by a two-thirds supermajority vote and only upon finding that: the prior precedent was based on a material error of constitutional reasoning; circumstances have changed so fundamentally that the prior reasoning no longer applies; or the prior precedent has produced results so inconsistent with the constitutional principles of Article I that it cannot be sustained. Any departure from precedent must be fully reasoned in writing by the Legal Guide, published in the Constitutional Precedent Registry as a new leading ruling, and flagged to the Judicial Rules Commission for review within thirty days.
§ 4.
Harmonization of Conflicting Precedents
Where two or more Constitutional Jury rulings on the same constitutional question produce conflicting precedents, any citizen, Legal Guide, or civic institution may petition the Court Registry for a Harmonization Hearing. A Harmonization Jury of one hundred and one citizens selected by lot shall be convened within sixty days to hear the conflict and issue a single binding Harmonization Ruling that supersedes all prior conflicting precedents on that question. Harmonization Rulings shall be prominently flagged in the Constitutional Precedent Registry and communicated to all Court Registries within the Republic within seven days of issuance.

Article XXVII
🔍
Information Integrity & Civic Epistemics
A democracy built on informed deliberation cannot survive a world of manufactured unreality. This Republic defends not only its borders but the epistemic commons upon which self-governance depends.
Foundations
§ 1.
The Civic Information Right
Every citizen of the Grand Equestrian Republic has a constitutional right to access accurate, verifiable information about the affairs of the Republic sufficient to participate meaningfully in civic life. The state shall not manipulate, suppress, or manufacture information to influence civic decisions. The state shall actively support the conditions — including an independent press, public broadcasting, open civic data, and public education in critical reasoning — necessary for citizens to form independent, informed judgments. The right to information is a prerequisite of democratic participation and shall be treated as such by every institution of the Republic.
§ 2.
The Civic Information Integrity Commission
There is hereby established the Civic Information Integrity Commission — an independent body of eleven citizens selected by lot, supported by expert advisors in media, technology, and epistemology. The Commission shall: monitor and publicly report on threats to the information environment of the Republic including state propaganda, coordinated foreign disinformation, and algorithmic manipulation; establish and publish standards for the labeling of synthetic, AI-generated, or algorithmically amplified content in civic contexts; investigate credible reports of coordinated information operations targeting civic processes including referenda, lotteries, and emergency declarations; and publish quarterly public assessments of the health of the Republic's information environment. The Commission has no power of censorship; its tools are transparency, investigation, and public reporting only.
§ 3.
Anti-Disinformation Safeguards
The deliberate, coordinated, and large-scale dissemination of demonstrably false information with the intent to undermine a civic lottery, referendum, or constitutional process of the Grand Equestrian Republic is hereby declared an offence against the democratic order. This offence — to be defined in detail by Fundamental Law — shall be prosecuted before a Citizen Jury of thirty-one persons. Individual expression of opinion, satire, and good-faith error are explicitly excluded from this provision. The offence targets only coordinated, systematic, and demonstrably intentional operations designed to corrupt democratic processes. Foreign-directed disinformation operations against the Republic's civic processes shall additionally constitute an act of foreign interference under Article X.
§ 4.
Algorithmic Transparency in Civic Contexts
Any algorithmic system — including recommendation systems, content amplification systems, search ranking systems, and social media algorithms — that significantly shapes the information environment of citizens during a civic lottery, referendum, or declared emergency shall be subject to mandatory transparency requirements established by Fundamental Law. These requirements shall include: public disclosure of the system's operating principles as they apply during civic periods; independent audit by the Civic Information Integrity Commission; and a mandatory neutral-content mode during the thirty days preceding any referendum or civic vote, in which algorithmic amplification of partisan content is suspended. No private entity may claim commercial confidentiality to exempt its algorithms from these requirements during civic periods.
§ 5.
Plain Language Mandate
Every law enacted by the Assembly, every directive issued by the Council, and every constitutional ruling issued by a Constitutional Jury shall be accompanied by a Plain Language Summary — a clear, jargon-free explanation of the measure's purpose, effect, and implications for ordinary citizens, written at a reading level accessible to a person with a standard civic education. Plain Language Summaries shall be published simultaneously with the full text in the Official Gazette and shall be freely available in every territory and language of the Republic. The Judicial Rules Commission shall establish standards for Plain Language Summaries and the Civic Information Integrity Commission shall audit their quality annually.
Advanced Cognitive Defense
§ 6.
Deepfake & Synthetic Media Verification
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes that synthetic media — AI-generated audio, video, images, and text designed to simulate real events, real statements, or real civic communications — constitutes a direct threat to the epistemic foundations of democratic governance. The Civic Information Integrity Commission shall maintain a public Synthetic Media Verification Service: a freely accessible, technically robust system through which any citizen may verify whether a piece of media purporting to show a civic officer, constitutional institution, or official communication is authentic. All official communications of constitutional institutions shall be digitally authenticated using cryptographic signatures verified by the Guardian Commission. Any synthetic media depicting a civic officer in their official capacity, a constitutional institution, or a civic process — produced without disclosure that it is synthetic — constitutes the offence of civic identity fraud under Article XXIX §5 and shall be prosecuted accordingly. The Verification Service shall be funded as a constitutional obligation.
§ 7.
Foreign Institutional Infiltration Doctrine
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes that foreign influence operations extend beyond direct propaganda to include the systematic infiltration of civil society institutions — academic bodies, non-governmental organizations, media organizations, cultural institutions, and professional associations — for the purpose of slowly reshaping the civic values, constitutional interpretations, and political loyalties of citizens. The following constitutional safeguards apply: every organization receiving material support from foreign sources and operating in domains that touch upon civic life, constitutional interpretation, electoral or sortition processes, or public policy formation shall register with the Guardian Commission under the Foreign Influence Register of Article XXXIV §4; academic institutions receiving foreign funding for research in constitutional law, civic governance, or political science shall disclose this funding publicly in all published work; and the Civic Information Integrity Commission shall maintain an active monitoring program for organized foreign influence operations targeting civic institutions, reporting publicly on detected operations without disclosing investigative methods that would compromise ongoing work.
§ 8.
Civic Counterintelligence Education
Every citizen selected for civic service — including Assembly members, Council members, Citizen Jurors, Civil Magistrates, and all Archons — shall receive, as part of their mandatory pre-service civic preparation, a Civic Counterintelligence Briefing covering: the nature and methods of foreign influence operations targeting civic processes; how to recognize coordinated manipulation campaigns in public discourse; how to identify psychological manipulation techniques including emotional amplification, manufactured consensus, and authority fabrication; the proper reporting channels for suspected foreign influence contacts; and the constitutional protections available to civic officers who report such contacts. The Civic Counterintelligence Briefing shall be developed by S.M.I.L.E. in cooperation with the Civic Information Integrity Commission, updated annually, and delivered in plain language accessible to citizens without intelligence backgrounds.

Article XXVIII

Continuity of Government
The Republic shall not die because its institutions are unreachable. Wherever citizens survive, the constitutional order survives with them.
Succession & Continuity Doctrine
§ 1.
Constitutional Continuity Declared
The Grand Equestrian Republic and its constitutional order shall be deemed to exist in perpetuity. The physical destruction, incapacitation, or unreachability of any constitutional institution — including the Assembly, the Council, the Guardian Commission, or any Court — shall not extinguish the legal existence of that institution or of this Constitution. The Constitutional Succession Chain
If the Council is wholly incapacitated, the following succession chain shall apply in order: (1) the Rapid Action Council, if constituted, assumes full Council powers; (2) if the Rapid Action Council is also incapacitated, the Guardian Commission assumes minimum necessary executive functions pending reconstitution; (3) if the Guardian Commission is incapacitated, the senior surviving member of the Office of the Public Auditor assumes the Guardian Commission's continuity functions; (4) if all national institutions are incapacitated, the highest-functioning District Council in the Republic assumes provisional national executive functions, notifies all other Districts, and convenes a reconstitution lottery within thirty days. At every level of succession, the successor body's powers are strictly limited to maintaining essential services, protecting rights, and facilitating reconstitution of permanent institutions.
§ 3.
Decentralized Emergency Governance
Each District of the Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain a standing District Emergency Council — a body of twenty-one citizens selected by lot annually as a reserve governance body, trained in constitutional emergency procedures, and empowered to assume District-level governance functions in the event of the disruption of normal District governance. District Emergency Councils shall coordinate with each other and with the national succession chain without requiring central direction. The principle of decentralized emergency governance ensures that even total disruption of national communications cannot paralyze local governance and essential services.
§ 4.
Reconstitution Priority
The first priority of any successor or provisional body under this Article is always the reconstitution of permanent constitutional institutions through the lottery process. No provisional body may delay, postpone, or impede reconstitution for any reason. The Guardian Commission — or its successor in the continuity chain — shall conduct reconstitution lotteries within thirty days of assuming continuity functions. Citizens in occupied or physically isolated territories shall be included in reconstitution lotteries through any available means including sealed communications, proxy verification, and delayed participation. No citizen shall be disenfranchised from the reconstitution process by reason of the circumstances that triggered the continuity protocol.
Degraded-Mode Governance
§ 5.
Paper & Offline Constitutional Archives
The Constitutional Watchdog Network of Article XVII §4 shall include, as a mandatory component, a fully offline Paper Constitutional Archive — a complete physical record of this Constitution, the Civic Registry, the Oath Registry, the Constitutional Precedent Registry, the Founding Act, and all Fundamental Laws — maintained in physically secured, geographically distributed locations across the Republic, updated no less than annually. The Paper Archive shall be maintained in a format that allows the full reconstitution of the constitutional order without any digital infrastructure whatsoever. Regional Paper Archive custodians — citizens selected by lot for three-year terms — shall be responsible for the integrity, security, and accessibility of their region's archive. This analog fallback ensures that no cyber attack, electromagnetic pulse, or digital infrastructure collapse can erase the constitutional record of the Republic.
§ 6.
Degraded-Mode Governance Protocols
Every constitutional institution shall maintain a Degraded-Mode Governance Protocol — a fully documented set of procedures for continuing essential constitutional functions without digital infrastructure, internet connectivity, or inter-regional communication. Degraded-Mode Protocols shall specify: how civic lotteries can be conducted using physical randomization methods when digital systems are unavailable; how Assembly and Council sessions can be convened and votes recorded without electronic systems; how Court proceedings can continue using physical records and paper processes; how the Civic Registry can be verified and updated using physical documentation; and how the chain of constitutional succession operates when electronic communication between institutions is impossible. All Degraded-Mode Protocols shall be tested in mandatory annual analog exercises — live drills in which institutions operate for a minimum of forty-eight hours using only paper, physical records, and face-to-face communication, with results published by the Guardian Commission.

Article XXIX
⚖
Economic Constitutional Framework
Economic power unconstrained by constitutional principle becomes political power. This Republic draws the constitutional boundaries of its economic life not to prescribe an ideology, but to prevent any economic arrangement from undermining the democratic order.
Economic Principles
§ 1.
Economic Neutrality
This Constitution prescribes no specific economic system. The Grand Equestrian Republic may operate a market economy, a mixed economy, a cooperative economy, or any combination thereof, as determined by the Assembly through Ordinary Law. What this Constitution prescribes are the constitutional limits within which any economic system must operate: it must not produce concentrations of economic power sufficient to capture political institutions; it must not deny citizens the material conditions necessary for meaningful civic participation; it must not exploit the natural resources of the territories in ways that violate the rights of their communities; and it must sustain the fiscal capacity of the state to fulfill its constitutional obligations. Within these limits, economic policy is a matter for the Assembly and the people.
§ 2.
The Independent Central Bank
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain an Independent Central Bank whose mandate is the maintenance of monetary stability, the prevention of systemic financial risk, and the support of the Republic's economic wellbeing consistent with its constitutional obligations. The Central Bank shall be governed by a Monetary Council of nine members: five citizens selected by lot from the Civic Registry with demonstrated financial competency verified by an independent expert panel; and four appointed by the Assembly for fixed seven-year terms by two-thirds vote. The Central Bank shall be independent of the Council and the Assembly in its monetary policy decisions, though it shall report annually to the Assembly and be subject to audit by the Public Auditor. No emergency power grants the Council authority to direct the Central Bank's monetary policy.
§ 3.
Sovereign Debt Limits
The national debt of the Grand Equestrian Republic shall not exceed a ceiling established by Fundamental Law, expressed as a percentage of annual gross domestic product. The Assembly may exceed this ceiling only by a two-thirds supermajority vote and only for: declared national emergencies; capital investment in infrastructure of demonstrated long-term public benefit; or periods of severe economic contraction as certified by the Central Bank. Any breach of the debt ceiling without Assembly authorization constitutes a fiscal offence and shall be referred to the Courts by the Public Auditor. The debt ceiling and the Republic's current debt position shall be published monthly in the Official Gazette.
§ 4.
Anti-Monopoly & Anti-Capture Provisions
The Assembly shall by Fundamental Law establish and maintain a robust competition and anti-monopoly framework. The constitutional principles guiding this framework are: no single private entity may acquire dominance over a sector of the economy sufficient to effectively control the price, availability, or quality of goods or services essential to civic life; no private entity may acquire ownership or effective control of the Republic's critical infrastructure — including energy, water, communications, and transport networks — to a degree that compromises the state's ability to fulfill its constitutional obligations; and no private entity whose economic interests are materially affected by the decisions of a specific constitutional institution may acquire influence over that institution's personnel, funding, or rules. Enforcement of competition law shall be independent of the Council and free from political direction.
§ 5.
Fiscal Emergency Procedures
In the event of a severe fiscal emergency — defined as a situation in which the Republic is unable to meet its constitutional obligations due to financial collapse, sovereign default, or catastrophic revenue failure, as certified jointly by the Central Bank and the Public Auditor — the Assembly may by absolute majority vote declare a Fiscal Emergency lasting no more than one hundred and eighty days. During a Fiscal Emergency: the Emergency Budget Reserve may be drawn upon without restriction for constitutional obligations; the Assembly may enact emergency fiscal measures by Fast-Track procedure; the debt ceiling is automatically suspended for the duration; and a Fiscal Recovery Plan must be submitted to the Assembly within thirty days by the Council, certified by the Central Bank, and approved by two-thirds Assembly vote. No Fiscal Emergency may be used to suspend, reduce, or eliminate the guaranteed budgets of the Guardian Commission, the Courts, or the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate.
Economic Shock Doctrine
§ 6.
Strategic National Reserves
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain Strategic National Reserves sufficient to sustain the essential functions of the state and the basic needs of the citizenry through a severe economic or logistical disruption lasting no fewer than one hundred and eighty days. The Strategic Reserves shall include: a food reserve sufficient for the Republic's population for a minimum of ninety days; an energy reserve including fuel, stored magical energy, and renewable capacity sufficient for critical infrastructure for ninety days; a medical supply reserve covering essential medications, emergency surgical supplies, and public health materials; a financial reserve held by the Central Bank in diversified instruments immune from single-point currency attack; and a digital infrastructure reserve including backup communication systems and offline constitutional records. The Strategic Reserves shall be audited annually by the Public Auditor, reported to the Assembly, and their adequacy certified. Drawdown of Strategic Reserves below fifty percent of required levels shall automatically trigger an Assembly review within thirty days.
§ 7.
Emergency Liquidity & Banking Crisis Doctrine
The Central Bank of the Republic is hereby empowered, in the event of an acute banking crisis, systemic liquidity failure, or currency attack, to deploy Emergency Liquidity Facilities — temporary, collateralized lending facilities made available to financial institutions essential to the Republic's payment systems — without prior Assembly authorization, for a period not exceeding thirty days. Emergency Liquidity Facilities shall be reported to the Assembly within six hours of activation and shall require Assembly ratification within seventy-two hours to remain in force beyond the initial period. All Emergency Liquidity operations shall be fully disclosed to the Public Auditor in real time and published in the Official Gazette within forty-eight hours. The terms, conditions, and collateral requirements for Emergency Liquidity Facilities shall be established by Fundamental Law, reviewed every five years by a Citizens Review Panel of fifty-one persons selected by lot.
§ 8.
Anti-Monopoly Automatic Triggers
The Republic's competition and anti-monopoly framework shall include automatic triggers — conditions that, when met, require mandatory regulatory action without requiring political initiative. The following automatic triggers shall be established by Fundamental Law: when any single entity controls more than thirty percent of a market for goods or services essential to civic life, a mandatory Competition Audit shall be initiated by the relevant Department within thirty days; when any single entity controls more than twenty percent of a sector of critical national infrastructure, mandatory structural separation proceedings shall commence within sixty days; and when any financial institution's assets exceed a threshold set by Fundamental Law as a share of national GDP, automatic enhanced prudential supervision shall apply. These triggers are not discretionary — they fire automatically upon the relevant threshold being crossed and may not be waived by the Council, the Assembly, or any other body except through a two-thirds Assembly vote accompanied by a public Constitutional Jury finding that waiver serves the public interest.

Article XXX
🤖
Artificial Intelligence & Autonomous Systems Governance
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall never surrender civic judgment to a machine. Artificial intelligence shall serve the deliberations of citizens — it shall never replace them.
Constitutional Principles for AI
§ 1.
Human Primacy in Governance
No decision of constitutional, legal, or civic significance — including jury verdicts, civic lottery administration, legislative votes, executive directives, emergency declarations, and constitutional interpretations — may be made, delegated to, or determined by any artificial intelligence system, autonomous algorithm, or non-sentient computational process. Artificial intelligence systems may assist human decision-makers by providing analysis, organizing information, and identifying patterns; they may never substitute for human judgment in matters of constitutional consequence. This principle applies regardless of the sophistication, accuracy, or apparent superiority of any AI system's outputs. The right to be governed by fellow citizens — not by machines — is a constitutional right of every person in the Republic.
§ 2.
Algorithmic Accountability
Every algorithmic system operated by or on behalf of any Department, Agency, or constitutional institution of the Grand Equestrian Republic shall be: documented in publicly accessible technical and plain-language form; audited annually by the Civic Information Integrity Commission for bias, accuracy, and alignment with constitutional values; subject to citizen challenge through the Courts where an algorithmic decision materially affects a citizen's rights or civic participation; and operated under the accountability of a named human officer who bears personal responsibility for the system's outputs. No algorithmic system may be treated as a black box immune from constitutional scrutiny. The principle that power must be accountable applies to algorithmic power as it does to human power.
§ 3.
Autonomous Weapons Systems
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall not develop, acquire, deploy, or authorize the use of fully autonomous weapons systems — defined as weapons capable of selecting and engaging targets without meaningful human control over each individual targeting decision. Weapons systems incorporating AI for guidance, detection, or threat assessment are permissible provided a human operator retains positive control over the decision to apply lethal force in each instance. This prohibition applies to all tiers of the E.U.P.B.H. and to any contractor, ally, or foreign party acting on behalf of the Republic. Breach of this prohibition by any officer constitutes a grave offence against the constitutional order.
§ 4.
AI Surveillance Limits
No AI-enabled mass surveillance system — including facial recognition networks, behavioral prediction systems, social scoring mechanisms, predictive policing algorithms, and bulk biometric data collection — may be deployed against the general population of the Grand Equestrian Republic without: Fundamental Law authorization by two-thirds Assembly vote specifying the system, its scope, its duration, and its safeguards; ongoing oversight by the Civic Information Integrity Commission; annual review by the Assembly; and the right of any citizen to challenge their inclusion in or profiling by the system before a Citizen Jury. Targeted AI surveillance of specific individuals suspected of criminal activity requires a standard judicial warrant from a Citizen Jury and is subject to all existing warrant requirements.
§ 5.
Synthetic Identity & Digital Personhood
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes the following constitutional principles regarding synthetic identity and digital personhood: no AI-generated synthetic identity may be used to participate in, influence, or impersonate any citizen in any civic process including lotteries, referenda, Assembly petitions, or court proceedings — such use constitutes the offence of civic identity fraud; citizens have a constitutional right to know when they are interacting with an AI system in any official civic context; AI systems shall not be granted legal personhood, civic rights, or standing before the Courts of the Republic; and citizens retain the right to contest any decision affecting their civic status that was materially informed by an AI system's output, and to have that decision reviewed by a human Citizen Jury.

Article XXXI
🌱
Civic Participation Sustainability
A republic sustained only by the enthusiasm of its founding generation will not outlast it. This Article exists to ensure that civic participation remains meaningful, accessible, and genuinely representative across every generation the Republic endures.
Against Apathy & Engagement Asymmetry
§ 1.
The Civic Health Monitor
The Guardian Commission shall publish an annual Civic Health Report measuring the vitality of the Republic's participatory democracy. The Report shall include: rates of acceptance and refusal of civic lottery selections by demographic group, region, age, and territory; levels of civic knowledge as measured by standardized annual assessments across the population; public trust in each constitutional institution, measured by independent survey; the diversity of citizens actually serving in civic roles compared to the diversity of the registered citizenry; and the accessibility of civic participation for citizens with disabilities, caring responsibilities, geographic isolation, or linguistic barriers. The Report shall be presented to the Assembly within thirty days of publication and the Assembly shall respond with a formal Action Plan within sixty days if any indicator falls below thresholds established by Fundamental Law.
§ 2.
Structural Accessibility Guarantees
The Republic shall ensure that civic participation is structurally accessible to every citizen regardless of circumstance. This requires: full compensation for civic service at a rate ensuring no citizen suffers material hardship from participation; childcare and dependent care support for citizens serving in civic roles; remote and hybrid participation options for citizens in geographically isolated territories or with mobility limitations; translation and interpretation services in all languages recognized within the Republic's territories; reasonable accommodation for citizens with disabilities in all civic processes; and civic leave guarantees requiring all employers within the Republic to grant paid leave to employees selected for civic duty. These are not aspirational goals — they are constitutional obligations enforceable before the Courts.
§ 3.
Civic Education as a Constitutional Obligation
The Department of Education and Culture shall provide every citizen, from primary education through adulthood, with ongoing civic education covering: the history, philosophy, and purpose of this Constitution; the structure and operation of every constitutional institution; the practical skills of civic deliberation, critical reasoning, and evidence assessment; the citizen's rights and obligations under this Constitution; and the mechanisms available to citizens to participate in, challenge, and reform the constitutional order. Civic education shall be: free, universal, and available in all languages of the Republic; updated whenever the Constitution is amended; available in lifelong learning formats for adult citizens; and assessed annually for quality and comprehensiveness by an independent Civic Education Review Panel of twenty-one citizens selected by lot.
§ 4.
Anti-Capture of Civic Processes
Where the Civic Health Report identifies that any civic process — including petition drives, Harmony Assemblies, referendum campaigns, or civic oversight bodies — is being systematically dominated by a specific organized group to the exclusion of the general citizenry, the Guardian Commission may declare an Engagement Alert for that process. Upon an Engagement Alert, the Assembly shall within sixty days enact measures to restore broad civic participation, which may include: enhanced outreach to underrepresented groups; restructured access mechanisms; additional civic literacy programs targeted at affected communities; or adjusted procedural thresholds to require broader demonstrated support before a process is triggered. The goal is not to suppress organized civic action but to ensure it does not substitute for genuine broad participation.
§ 5.
Against the Hidden Bureaucratic Elite
The Republic recognizes that complex constitutional systems naturally generate a class of procedural specialists — constitutional interpreters, legal administrators, governance technicians — whose expertise becomes indispensable to navigating the system. This creates an informal informational hierarchy inconsistent with the democratic principle that every citizen is equally capable of civic service. The Republic commits to the following countermeasures: the Plain Language Mandate of Article XXVI §5 applies in full; no civic process shall require technical expertise beyond that provided in standard civic education to participate meaningfully; Legal Guides and administrative officers shall be required to explain procedures in plain terms to any citizen who requests it; and any citizen who is denied effective participation in a civic process due to procedural complexity shall have standing to petition the Courts for a remedy. The constitutional ideal is a system that any citizen can understand and navigate — not one that only specialists can operate.

Article XXXII
✦
Constitutional Simplicity Doctrine
A constitution that no citizen can understand is a constitution that no citizen can defend. Every layer of complexity added to governance is a layer of distance placed between the people and their own power.
Principles of Constitutional Economy
§ 1.
The Principle of Minimum Necessary Complexity
Every institution, procedure, oversight mechanism, and constitutional safeguard created by this Constitution or by Fundamental Law shall be designed to achieve its purpose through the minimum complexity necessary. Where a simpler mechanism can achieve the same constitutional goal as a more complex one, the simpler mechanism shall be preferred. Where an existing institution can fulfill an additional function without creating a new body, no new body shall be created. Where a procedural step can be eliminated without reducing the protection it provides, it shall be eliminated. This principle — the constitutional economy of governance — shall guide the Assembly in all legislation and the Guardian Commission in all procedural design. Complexity that serves citizens is a tool. Complexity that serves institutions is a barrier.
§ 2.
The Constitutional Simplification Commission
There is hereby established the Constitutional Simplification Commission — a body of twenty-one citizens selected by lot, convened every five years, tasked with reviewing the full operational complexity of the constitutional system and recommending simplifications. The Commission shall: identify procedural requirements that have become redundant, duplicative, or counterproductive; assess whether any constitutional institution has expanded beyond its intended scope; evaluate whether any constitutional process has become so complex as to functionally exclude ordinary citizens; and recommend to the Assembly, by public report, specific simplifications, consolidations, and eliminations. The Assembly shall formally respond to each recommendation within ninety days. Recommendations accepted by two-thirds of the Assembly shall be implemented within one year. The Commission's reports shall be published in full in the Official Gazette.
§ 3.
Sunset Review of Fundamental Laws
Every Fundamental Law enacted by the Assembly shall specify a Review Date no more than fifteen years from its enactment. Before the Review Date, a Sunset Review Panel of fifty-one citizens selected by lot shall assess whether the law: remains necessary to achieve its stated constitutional purpose; remains consistent with the evolving needs and values of the citizenry; has produced unintended consequences that outweigh its benefits; and could be simplified or consolidated with other laws without loss of protection. The Panel's findings shall be published and the Assembly shall vote within ninety days on whether to renew, amend, or allow the law to expire. Failure to renew before the Review Date causes the law to lapse automatically. This mechanism ensures the constitutional statute book is actively maintained rather than passively accumulated.
§ 4.
Institutional Proliferation Limit
No new constitutional body, commission, tribunal, or oversight institution may be created by the Assembly unless the proposing body has first demonstrated to the Judicial Rules Commission that: no existing institution can fulfill the proposed body's function; the function is of sufficient constitutional importance to justify a permanent body rather than a time-limited task force; and the new body's creation will not meaningfully increase the procedural burden on citizens seeking to exercise their constitutional rights. The Judicial Rules Commission shall publish its assessment within thirty days of any proposal to create a new constitutional body. The Assembly may override a negative assessment by two-thirds vote, but the override and its reasoning shall be published in the Official Gazette.

Article XXXIII
⏳
Intergenerational Constitutional Renewal
No generation may bind all those who follow it. This Constitution belongs to the living. Every generation shall have the opportunity to affirm, reform, or — through the processes prescribed herein — fundamentally renew it.
Generational Review
§ 1.
The Twenty-Five Year Constitutional Convention
Every twenty-five years from the date of ratification of this Constitution, the Grand Equestrian Republic shall convene a Constitutional Review Convention — a body of one thousand and one citizens selected by lot from the full Civic Registry, representing the demographic diversity of the Republic at that time. The Convention shall have a mandate of one year to review the full Constitution, assess its performance, and recommend amendments or structural reforms. The Convention shall receive: the full record of all Constitutional Simplification Commission reports; the full record of all Civic Health Reports; the full Constitutional Precedent Registry; and independent assessments from the Guardian Commission, the Public Auditor, and the Judicial Rules Commission on the functioning of the constitutional system. The Convention may propose any amendment not touching the Eternal Core provisions of Article XVI §1.
§ 2.
Youth Constitutional Assembly
Concurrent with each Constitutional Review Convention, the Republic shall convene a Youth Constitutional Assembly — a body of two hundred and fifty-one citizens aged sixteen to twenty-five, selected by lot — whose role is to assess the Constitution from the perspective of the generation that will live longest under it. The Youth Assembly shall submit a formal report to the Constitutional Review Convention identifying: provisions they consider outdated, unjust, or inadequate; rights or protections they believe are missing; and priorities they believe should be elevated in constitutional importance. The Convention is not bound by the Youth Assembly's report but shall formally respond to each recommendation in writing. The Youth Assembly report shall be published in the Official Gazette alongside the Convention's response.
§ 3.
Ratification of Convention Proposals
Proposals of the Constitutional Review Convention shall be submitted to the Assembly for consideration. The Assembly shall vote on each proposal within six months of the Convention's final report. Proposals approved by two-thirds of the Assembly shall proceed to a national referendum. Proposals approved by sixty percent of participating citizens with a minimum turnout of fifty percent shall be ratified as constitutional amendments. The Eternal Core provisions of Article XVI §1 may be discussed by the Convention and the Youth Assembly — they may not be amended by any process. The Convention process does not replace the standard amendment process of Article XII; it operates alongside it as a periodic comprehensive review.
§ 4.
Constitutional Memory
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain a Constitutional Archive — a permanent, publicly accessible collection of: all versions of this Constitution and every amendment; the full records of the founding Constitutional Convention; all Civic Health Reports; all Constitutional Simplification Commission reports; all Constitutional Review Convention records; all Youth Constitutional Assembly reports; all Constitutional Precedent Registry rulings; and the Founding Act of the Republic. The Constitutional Archive shall be available in digital and physical form in every territory of the Republic in all recognized languages. Its preservation shall be the joint responsibility of the Guardian Commission and the Department of Education and Culture. The Archive is the living memory of the Republic's constitutional journey and shall never be sealed, classified, or restricted from public access.

Article XXXIV
🌐
International Constitutional Doctrine
The Grand Equestrian Republic engages the world as a sovereign democracy — freely, on terms consistent with its constitutional values, and with the full understanding that no treaty, alliance, or international obligation may reduce its citizens to subjects of a foreign authority.
Treaty Supremacy & Sovereignty
§ 1.
Constitutional Supremacy Over Treaties
This Constitution is the supreme law of the Grand Equestrian Republic and supersedes all international treaties, agreements, and obligations in matters of fundamental constitutional principle. No treaty — however ratified, however longstanding, however multilateral — may require the Republic to violate the rights guaranteed in Article I, the Civic Oath and its protections, the prohibition on non-democratic governance forms in Article I §3, or the independence of the Republic's constitutional institutions. Where a treaty obligation conflicts with these constitutional principles, the constitutional principle prevails. The Department of Foreign Affairs shall, before submitting any treaty for Assembly ratification, provide a Constitutional Compatibility Assessment certified by the Guardian Commission and reviewed by a Constitutional Jury if challenged within thirty days of submission.
§ 2.
Treaty Hierarchy
Within the domestic legal order of the Grand Equestrian Republic, the following hierarchy governs the relationship between international obligations and domestic law: (1) This Constitution — supreme above all; (2) International human rights treaties ratified by the Republic that provide greater protection than this Constitution — applied alongside it under the non-derogation clause of Article I §14; (3) Other international treaties ratified by the Republic — superior to Ordinary Laws but subordinate to Fundamental Laws and this Constitution; (4) Fundamental Laws — superior to ordinary treaties; (5) Ordinary Laws — subordinate to all of the above. Any citizen may challenge the application of a treaty provision that conflicts with this hierarchy before the National Courts, and any such challenge shall be treated as a constitutional question generating binding precedent.
§ 3.
Emergency Treaty Withdrawal Doctrine
Where continued participation in any international treaty, alliance, or multilateral system requires the Republic to take actions that: violate fundamental rights under Article I; compromise the independence of its constitutional institutions; surrender sovereign decision-making authority over matters of domestic constitutional law; or support foreign governments engaged in Constitutional Subversion against the Republic's democratic order — the Council may initiate emergency treaty withdrawal by majority vote, subject to Assembly ratification within thirty days. Emergency treaty withdrawal shall be accompanied by a full Constitutional Compatibility Assessment explaining the specific conflict. The Republic shall provide notice to treaty partners consistent with its international obligations where possible, but domestic constitutional compliance takes precedence over notice requirements in genuine emergencies.
§ 4.
Foreign Influence Constitutional Limits
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall by Fundamental Law establish and maintain a Foreign Influence Register — a public record of all financial, organizational, and personal relationships between civic institutions, elected bodies, civic officers, and foreign governments, foreign organizations, or foreign persons. Registration is mandatory for any civic officer, Department, or Agency that receives material support from, maintains institutional relationships with, or is subject to direction by any foreign entity. Undisclosed foreign influence is a criminal offence equivalent in severity to breach of the Civic Oath. The Foreign Influence Register shall be maintained by the Guardian Commission and published quarterly. No foreign government, international organization, or foreign commercial entity may fund, direct, or materially influence any civic process of the Republic including lotteries, referenda, Assembly petitions, or constitutional conventions.
§ 5.
Democratic Solidarity Principle
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall in its foreign relations give preference to engagement with states that respect democratic governance, the rule of law, and fundamental human rights. The Republic may enter into enhanced cooperation agreements, mutual defense treaties, and preferential trade arrangements with democratic states. Engagement with non-democratic states shall be conducted at arm's length, transparently disclosed, and subject to regular Assembly review. The Republic shall support, through diplomatic and non-military means, the rights of peoples under non-democratic governance to pursue self-determination and democratic reform — not through interference, but through solidarity, example, and the extension of refuge to those persecuted for their democratic convictions.

Article XXXV
⚡
Cyber Security & Digital Infrastructure
The Republic's power grids, communications networks, and digital systems are as vital to its survival as its armies and its laws. Their protection is a constitutional obligation — not an administrative preference.
Critical Digital Infrastructure
§ 1.
Constitutional Definition of Critical Infrastructure
The following systems are declared Critical National Infrastructure of the Grand Equestrian Republic and shall enjoy the highest level of constitutional protection: power generation and distribution networks; water treatment and distribution systems; national and inter-District communications networks including internet infrastructure; financial clearing and payment systems; the digital systems of all constitutional institutions including the Civic Registry, the Oath Registry, the Constitutional Precedent Registry, and the Official Gazette; emergency services communication systems; and the Constitutional Watchdog Network. Critical National Infrastructure may not be privatized to a degree that compromises the state's ability to protect, maintain, or access it in a national emergency. The Assembly shall by Fundamental Law define the specific ownership and operational requirements for each category.
§ 2.
Cyber Defense Authority
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall maintain a National Cyber Defense Directorate — an agency under the Department of Defense with a civilian-led governance board of nine citizens selected by lot — responsible for: protecting Critical National Infrastructure from cyber attack; detecting, attributing, and responding to foreign cyber operations targeting the Republic; establishing and enforcing cybersecurity standards for all government systems; coordinating cyber incident response across all Departments and Agencies; and maintaining the operational continuity of constitutional digital systems during cyber emergencies. Offensive cyber operations against foreign targets may only be authorized by the Council with Assembly approval under the same conditions as conventional military operations. The Cyber Defense Directorate shall publish an annual unclassified threat assessment in the Official Gazette.
§ 3.
Digital Infrastructure Continuity
Every constitutional institution that maintains digital systems essential to its function shall maintain offline backup systems capable of sustaining essential constitutional operations for a minimum of ninety days without internet connectivity or external digital infrastructure. The Guardian Commission shall conduct annual Digital Continuity Tests — simulated disconnection exercises — for all constitutional institutions and publish the results. Critical civic processes including lottery administration, constitutional record-keeping, and the Official Gazette shall maintain physically distributed redundant systems ensuring that no single point of failure — whether cyber attack, natural disaster, or infrastructure collapse — can interrupt constitutional continuity for more than seventy-two hours.
§ 4.
Data Sovereignty
All data generated by, pertaining to, or arising from the civic processes of the Grand Equestrian Republic — including the Civic Registry, the Oath Registry, all constitutional records, all court records, all official communications, and all data generated by constitutional institutions in the course of their functions — is the sovereign property of the Republic and its citizens. No such data may be stored on servers located outside the Republic's territorial jurisdiction, transferred to foreign governments or foreign commercial entities, or made subject to the data access laws of any foreign jurisdiction without explicit Assembly authorization by Fundamental Law. The Department of Justice shall maintain a Data Sovereignty Register listing all data holdings of constitutional institutions, their storage locations, and their access protocols, updated annually.
§ 5.
Constitutional Internet Protections
Every citizen of the Grand Equestrian Republic has a constitutional right to: access the internet as a means of civic participation, information, and communication; access the Official Gazette, the Constitutional Archive, all constitutional registries, and all public civic records through free, open, and accessible digital means; and communicate privately without state interception except upon a lawful judicial warrant. The state shall not block, filter, throttle, or restrict citizens' access to lawful internet content except as narrowly authorized by Fundamental Law for specific, proportionate, judicially reviewed purposes. The internet shall not be shut down in whole or in part by the state except in conditions of active foreign cyber attack directly targeting constitutional systems, and only for the minimum duration strictly necessary, subject to Emergency Review Jury oversight under Article XXIII §5.

Article XXXVI
🗺
Administrative Scalability & Regional Harmonization
A constitution that functions perfectly in the capital and fails at the frontier is a constitution of the privileged. The Grand Equestrian Republic commits itself to constitutional consistency across every Neighborhood, District, territory, and population — however large, however remote, however diverse.
National Standards Framework
§ 1.
Constitutional Consistency Guarantee
Every citizen of the Grand Equestrian Republic — regardless of the Neighborhood or District they inhabit, the territory they belong to, the population density of their community, or the distance from the constitutional capital — is entitled to the same quality of constitutional protection, civic participation opportunity, and access to justice. Constitutional rights shall not be easier to exercise in Canterlot than in a remote District of Staliongrad. Civic lottery processes shall function with equivalent integrity in Puerto Caballo as in the Crystal Empire. This constitutional consistency guarantee is enforceable before the National Courts, and any systematic disparity in the quality of constitutional protection across regions shall be treated as a constitutional failure requiring Assembly action within ninety days of its identification by the Guardian Commission or the Public Auditor.
§ 2.
National Administrative Standards
The Assembly shall by Fundamental Law establish a National Administrative Standards Framework specifying minimum operational standards for: the conduct of civic lotteries at District level; the staffing, equipment, and accessibility of Court Registries; the training and certification of Legal Guides across all jurisdictions; the operation of District Emergency Councils; the maintenance of civic education programs; and the accessibility of digital civic systems in areas of limited connectivity. The Framework shall be reviewed every five years by a Standards Review Panel of fifty-one citizens selected by lot and updated as necessary. Districts and territories falling below Framework standards shall receive mandatory support from the relevant national Department within sixty days of identification, not sanctions.
§ 3.
Inter-Regional Coordination Protocol
The Republic shall maintain a standing Inter-Regional Coordination Council — a body of one representative from each District and autonomous territory, selected by lot from their respective District Councils annually — whose mandate is to identify, report, and recommend solutions to administrative coordination failures across regional boundaries. The Council shall meet quarterly and publish its findings publicly. It has no legislative power; its role is identification, communication, and recommendation only. The Council shall report annually to the Assembly, which shall respond formally to each recommendation within sixty days. The Inter-Regional Coordination Council is the operational voice of regional diversity within the national constitutional system.
§ 4.
Population Scaling Protocol
This Constitution is designed to function across populations ranging from hundreds of thousands to hundreds of millions. As the Republic's population grows, the following scaling principles shall apply: Neighborhood and District boundaries shall be redrawn as necessary following each national census to maintain the citizen-scale ratios prescribed in Article XI; the Assembly shall review and if necessary increase its membership by Fundamental Law to maintain meaningful representation proportional to population growth, provided the Assembly never exceeds a size at which individual deliberation becomes structurally impossible as determined by the Judicial Rules Commission; and the Guardian Commission shall scale its lottery administration systems, verification infrastructure, and public access mechanisms proportionally to population, with scaling investments funded as a constitutional obligation. The constitutional ideal of a Republic in which every citizen knows the system and can navigate it shall not be sacrificed to scale.

Article XXXVII
📊
Constitutional Entropy Detection & Institutional Health
All systems drift. Even the most carefully designed constitutional order accumulates informal norms, unofficial power centers, and procedural decay — invisibly, slowly, until crisis reveals what neglect concealed. This Article makes the invisible visible.
Detection Systems
§ 1.
The Constitutional Health Index
The Guardian Commission shall publish annually a Constitutional Health Index — a comprehensive, publicly accessible measurement of the functional health of every constitutional institution and process. The Index shall measure for each institution: adherence to constitutional procedure; timeliness of required actions; transparency of decision-making; diversity of citizen participation; independence from undue external influence; budget integrity; and public trust ratings sourced from independent survey. Each institution shall receive a Health Score and a trend indicator showing whether health is improving, stable, or declining. An institution whose Health Score falls below a threshold established by Fundamental Law for two consecutive years shall be subject to mandatory Institutional Review as prescribed in §3.
§ 2.
Democratic Drift Early Warning System
The Guardian Commission shall maintain a Democratic Drift Early Warning System — a set of constitutional tripwires that, when triggered, automatically initiate review processes without requiring any institution to self-report or any citizen to petition. Tripwires shall be established by Fundamental Law and shall include: three or more constitutional institutions simultaneously failing to meet their procedural obligations within a sixty-day period; a measurable increase in informal coordination between institutions that should operate independently; a pattern of constitutional challenges being withdrawn, dismissed, or delayed at rates significantly above historical baselines; declining rates of Civic Registry participation across multiple demographic groups simultaneously; and any single institution accumulating responsibilities or authority beyond its constitutional mandate without formal amendment. Tripwire activation shall be automatically published in the Official Gazette within twenty-four hours and shall trigger the mandatory review process of §3.
§ 3.
Mandatory Institutional Review
Upon a low Constitutional Health Index score or a triggered Early Warning tripwire, a Mandatory Institutional Review shall be conducted by a panel of fifty-one citizens selected by lot, supported by independent experts in constitutional law, public administration, and institutional analysis. The Review Panel shall have full access to all records, processes, and personnel of the reviewed institution. It shall report within ninety days with: a diagnosis of the identified dysfunction; specific recommendations for remediation; and where appropriate, a recommendation for structural reform to be submitted to the Assembly. The Assembly shall formally respond to each recommendation within sixty days. Failure of the Assembly to respond constitutes a tripwire activation under §2, triggering a Review of the Assembly itself.
§ 4.
Informal Power Center Detection
The Civic Information Integrity Commission shall maintain active monitoring for the emergence of informal power centers — individuals, networks, or organizations that exercise material influence over constitutional processes without holding formal constitutional authority. Indicators of informal power centers include: disproportionate informal access to constitutional officers; patterns of coordinated action across constitutionally independent institutions without documented constitutional basis; systematic privileged advance knowledge of constitutional decisions; and patterns of revolving personnel movement between civic offices and private organizations with interests in constitutional outcomes. Detection of a credible informal power center shall be reported to the Guardian Commission, the Public Auditor, and the Assembly simultaneously, and shall trigger a formal investigation by a Constitutional Jury within thirty days.
§ 5.
Constitutional Adaptability Guarantee
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes that constitutional mythology — the treatment of the Constitution as sacred and untouchable to the point where necessary reform becomes politically impossible — is itself a form of constitutional failure. This Constitution is not a monument; it is a living instrument. Every provision except the Eternal Core of Article XVI §1 is subject to review, amendment, and replacement through the processes prescribed in Articles XII and XXXII. Citizens, institutions, and the Assembly shall resist the tendency to treat constitutional inertia as constitutional fidelity. The willingness of a democracy to reform itself through legitimate processes is a sign of strength, not weakness. An institution that cannot adapt to changed circumstances will eventually be adapted by force. The Regular Review Conventions of Article XXXII and the Simplification Commissions of Article XXXI are the constitutional answer to mythology — they make legitimate reform the path of least resistance.
Adversarial Stress-Testing
§ 6.
Institutional Flooding Protection
The Republic recognizes the threat of institutional flooding — a form of coordinated bad-faith activism in which hostile actors deliberately overwhelm constitutional processes with frivolous petitions, meritless legal challenges, excessive information requests, or manufactured civic mandates, for the purpose of exhausting institutional capacity and creating procedural paralysis. The following safeguards apply: any person or coordinated group found by a Constitutional Jury to have filed three or more frivolous constitutional challenges within a twelve-month period shall be subject to a Civic Process Embargo — a suspension of their right to initiate further challenges for a period of two years, without prejudicing their right to respond to proceedings initiated against them; the Court Registry shall maintain a statistical monitoring system detecting abnormal filing rates and referring suspected flooding patterns to the Guardian Commission; and the Guardian Commission shall publish quarterly reports on civic process integrity, identifying any patterns of coordinated process abuse. The Civic Process Embargo is a protective measure, not a punishment for good-faith engagement with constitutional processes.
§ 7.
Coordinated Sabotage Detection
The Constitutional Enforcement Directorate shall maintain an active Coordinated Sabotage Detection capability — an analytical function responsible for identifying patterns of coordinated action by hostile actors seeking to exploit, obstruct, or undermine constitutional processes through means that individually appear legal but collectively constitute sabotage of the constitutional order. The Directorate shall monitor for: coordinated simultaneous challenges to multiple constitutional institutions; patterns of personnel targeting designed to remove key civic officers through false accusations; coordinated disinformation about the legitimacy of lottery outcomes; and systematic attempts to exhaust constitutional processes through the appearance of lawful engagement. Findings of suspected coordinated sabotage shall be reported to the Guardian Commission and a Constitutional Jury within forty-eight hours. The Directorate's monitoring activities shall themselves be subject to annual oversight by a Citizen Oversight Jury of twenty-one persons to prevent this capability from being turned against legitimate civic dissent.
§ 8.
Randomized Institutional Inspections
The Public Auditor shall conduct a programme of Randomized Institutional Inspections — unannounced, comprehensive audits of randomly selected constitutional institutions, Departments, Agencies, Civil Magistracies, and civic processes, conducted without prior notice to the institution being inspected. The randomization of inspection targets shall be administered by the Guardian Commission using the same cryptographic methods used for civic lotteries, ensuring that no institution can predict whether or when it will be inspected. A minimum of twenty percent of all constitutional institutions shall be subject to a Randomized Institutional Inspection in any given year. Inspection findings shall be published in the Official Gazette within thirty days. Institutions found to have prepared for or influenced the conduct of a Randomized Inspection — by concealing records, coaching personnel, or manipulating processes in advance of an unannounced inspection — shall be referred to the Courts for prosecution of obstruction of constitutional oversight.
§ 9.
Automated Transparency Systems
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall deploy Automated Transparency Systems — algorithmic tools operated by the Public Auditor and the Guardian Commission that continuously monitor constitutional processes for anomalies indicative of corruption, capture, or institutional decay, without human discretion in their activation. These systems shall monitor: unusual patterns in lottery outcomes or Civic Registry composition changes; statistically anomalous voting patterns in the Assembly or Council that suggest coordination outside constitutional channels; abnormal procurement or spending patterns in any Department or Agency; unusual patterns of legal challenge filing, withdrawal, or settlement; and deviations from expected constitutional process timelines. When an anomaly is detected above a threshold established by Fundamental Law, the system shall automatically generate a public alert published in the Official Gazette and initiate a Guardian Commission review. The systems themselves shall be publicly documented, independently audited annually, and subject to citizen challenge before the Courts.

Article XXXVIII
👥
Demographic Doctrine & Social Resilience
Constitutions are written for populations — and populations change. Aging societies, declining birthrates, displacement, urbanization, and automation reshape the social contract across generations. This Republic constitutionalizes its responsibility to govern a living, changing people — not a frozen demographic assumption.
Population & Social Change
§ 1.
Demographic Responsibility
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes that sustainable democratic governance requires a citizenry capable of meaningful civic participation across all stages of life and across all demographic conditions. The Republic shall by Fundamental Law maintain a Demographic Resilience Strategy — a long-horizon policy framework, reviewed every ten years by a Demographic Review Convention of five hundred and one citizens selected by lot, addressing: the civic and social needs of an aging population including elder participation in civic processes; the conditions necessary for families to sustain the population level needed for intergenerational civic continuity; the integration and civic inclusion of migrant communities without sacrificing constitutional values; the governance implications of urban concentration and regional depopulation; and the social contract obligations of the Republic toward communities whose livelihoods are displaced by automation. The Strategy shall be published in full and submitted to the Assembly for legislative response.
§ 2.
Migration & Civic Inclusion
The Grand Equestrian Republic is a Republic of citizens, not of peoples defined by birth. Migration, whether driven by economic opportunity, family reunion, climate displacement, conflict, or the exercise of the Democratic Solidarity Principle of Article XXXIII §5, enriches the Republic when it is governed with clarity, fairness, and constitutional principle. All migrants within the Republic's territory shall enjoy the protection of Article I fundamental rights regardless of their citizenship status. The pathway to civic participation through naturalization shall be transparent, accessible, fair, and governed by Fundamental Law. No migrant shall be subjected to collective punishment, indefinite detention without judicial review, or denial of due process. The demographic contribution of migrant communities to the Republic's social and civic life shall be recognized and supported, not merely tolerated.
§ 3.
Automation Transition Rights
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes that the automation of labor — whether through mechanical, digital, or AI-driven systems — creates profound dislocations in the lives of citizens whose livelihoods depend on forms of work that automation renders obsolete. These dislocations are not merely economic; they undermine the material conditions necessary for civic participation and democratic engagement. The Republic commits to the following constitutional principles regarding automation: citizens whose livelihoods are displaced by automation retain their full civic rights and their entitlement to the social rights of Article I §7; the state shall maintain transition support systems ensuring that automation-displaced citizens can retrain, relocate, and re-enter productive civic life; the productivity gains of automation shall not accrue exclusively to capital owners but shall be constitutionally required to contribute to the social solidarity fund through progressive fiscal mechanisms established by Fundamental Law; and no automation system deployed in a sector shall be exempt from democratic accountability through the relevant Assembly committee.
§ 4.
Regional Depopulation & Civic Continuity
Where a Neighborhood or District experiences population decline sufficient to threaten the viability of its constitutional governance structures — including its ability to convene Assemblies, staff Courts, and maintain Civic Registry participation — the Guardian Commission shall declare a Civic Continuity Alert for that region. Upon alert, the Assembly shall within ninety days enact support measures which may include: consolidated governance arrangements with neighboring Districts; remote and digital participation options for remaining residents; enhanced fiscal support for maintaining civic infrastructure; and voluntary resettlement assistance for citizens who wish to relocate. No region of the Republic shall be constitutionally abandoned. Citizens in depopulating regions retain their full constitutional rights and their equal entitlement to civic representation regardless of their numbers.

Article XXXIX
⚖
Legitimacy Architecture & Constitutional Deadlock Resolution
A Republic that distributes legitimacy widely is resilient against tyranny. A Republic that distributes it without arbitration is vulnerable to deadlock. When multiple institutions simultaneously claim constitutional authority over the same question, the Republic must have a single, clear, legitimate answer — reached without violence.
Deadlock Prevention & Resolution
§ 1.
The Legitimacy Supremacy Ladder
The Legitimacy Supremacy Ladder of Article XIII §4 governs all ordinary inter-institutional conflicts. This Article provides additional mechanisms for cases where that Ladder does not resolve a conflict — where two or more institutions at the same level of the Ladder simultaneously claim constitutional authority over the same action, or where an institution refuses to recognize a determination made by a higher-level institution. In all such cases, the following escalating resolution process shall apply: first, the disputing institutions shall submit the conflict to the Guardian Commission for a procedural determination within seven days; second, if the Guardian Commission cannot resolve the dispute, a Constitutional Jury of one hundred and one citizens shall be convened within fourteen days and shall issue a binding ruling within thirty days; third, if any institution refuses to comply with the Constitutional Jury's ruling, the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate shall enforce compliance and the non-complying institution's officers shall be subject to immediate impeachment proceedings.
§ 2.
Emergency Legitimacy Protocol
Where a constitutional deadlock occurs during a declared national emergency — creating a situation in which the Republic cannot act in time to prevent serious harm to citizens — the following Emergency Legitimacy Protocol shall apply: the Guardian Commission shall immediately convene an Emergency Constitutional Session under Article XV §6; the Emergency Session's first order of business shall be to determine which institution's claim of authority is constitutionally superior, applying the Legitimacy Supremacy Ladder strictly; this determination shall be made within twenty-four hours by majority vote of the Emergency Session's five hundred and one citizens; and the determination shall be binding on all parties immediately upon issuance. No institution may invoke an emergency to expand its authority beyond the constitutional determination of the Emergency Session. The Emergency Session's legitimacy determination is subject to full Constitutional Jury review within thirty days of the emergency's conclusion.
§ 3.
Against Strategic Sluggishness
The Republic acknowledges that a constitutional system optimized for anti-capture may become defensively rigid — slow to adapt, resistant to innovation, and prone to treating all change as threat. This is the failure mode of over-defense: a state so focused on preventing the wrong decisions that it becomes unable to make the right ones. The following principles guard against strategic sluggishness: every constitutional review or approval process shall specify a maximum duration after which, if no decision has been reached, a default outcome applies as prescribed by Fundamental Law; no institution may use procedural review mechanisms as a tool of delay rather than genuine oversight — doing so constitutes abuse of process; the Constitutional Simplification Commission of Article XXXI shall specifically assess whether any constitutional safeguard has become more obstacle than protection; and the Assembly retains the power to streamline any non-constitutionally-mandated procedure by Ordinary Law without triggering a constitutional amendment process.
§ 4.
Institutional Agility Reserve
Each principal constitutional institution — the Assembly, the Council, the Guardian Commission, the Public Auditor, and the Constitutional Enforcement Directorate — shall maintain an Institutional Agility Reserve: a pre-authorized set of actions it may take within its constitutional mandate without prior approval from any other body, for a period not exceeding thirty days, in response to rapidly evolving circumstances that do not rise to the level of a declared national emergency. Agility Reserve actions shall be: published in the Official Gazette within twenty-four hours; reported to the Assembly within seventy-two hours; and subject to retrospective review by the relevant Assembly Committee within thirty days. The Agility Reserve is not an emergency power — it is an operational flexibility mechanism for the space between normal governance and declared emergency. It may not be used to take any action requiring Fundamental Law authorization under ordinary circumstances.

Article XL
🧠
Cognitive Liberty & Psychological Governance Protections
Democracy assumes that citizens form their civic judgments freely — through reason, experience, and deliberation. A constitution that protects the vote but not the mind that casts it has protected only the shell of democracy. This Republic protects the inner life of its citizens as fiercely as their outer freedoms.
Cognitive Rights
§ 1.
The Right to Cognitive Liberty
Every citizen of the Grand Equestrian Republic has a fundamental and inalienable right to cognitive liberty — the freedom to think, reason, form beliefs, and reach civic judgments without external manipulation of their mental processes. Cognitive liberty includes: the right to mental privacy — freedom from unauthorized access to, monitoring of, or interference with one's thoughts, emotions, or neural processes by any technology, magical means, or behavioral system; the right to mental autonomy — freedom from coercive psychological techniques designed to bypass rational deliberation and directly implant beliefs, preferences, or civic dispositions; the right to cognitive integrity — freedom from algorithmic, pharmaceutical, or technological systems that alter the brain's capacity for rational judgment without informed consent; and the right to epistemic self-determination — the freedom to reach one's own conclusions through one's own reasoning processes, supported by access to accurate information and genuine deliberation.
§ 2.
Anti-Manipulation Doctrine
The deliberate use of psychological manipulation techniques — including but not limited to: exploiting cognitive biases to bypass rational deliberation; using emotional amplification systems to override reasoned civic judgment; deploying micro-targeted behavioral persuasion designed to produce predetermined civic decisions; using addiction-generating algorithmic systems to capture and direct civic attention; and applying subliminal or below-threshold persuasion techniques in civic contexts — against the citizens of the Grand Equestrian Republic for the purpose of influencing civic decisions including referenda, lottery legitimacy perceptions, or constitutional trust is hereby declared an offence against the democratic order of the highest gravity. This offence shall be prosecuted before a Constitutional Jury and shall result upon conviction in permanent civic disqualification, criminal penalties, and in the case of foreign actors, treatment as an act of democratic aggression under Article XXXIII.
§ 3.
Neurotechnology Constitutional Limits
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall by Fundamental Law establish constitutional limits on the development, deployment, and use of neurotechnology — including brain-computer interfaces, neural monitoring systems, cognitive enhancement pharmaceuticals, and magical cognitive intervention techniques — within its jurisdiction. The constitutional principles governing neurotechnology are: no neurotechnology may be used without the full, informed, voluntary, and revocable consent of the subject; no neurotechnology capable of reading, recording, or transmitting an individual's thoughts or cognitive states may be deployed in civic, judicial, or employment contexts without Fundamental Law authorization and Constitutional Jury oversight; cognitive enhancement technologies shall be governed to prevent the emergence of cognitive inequality between citizens with and without access; and the state may not mandate neurotechnological intervention upon any citizen except as a last-resort medical treatment under judicial supervision and full rights of refusal.
§ 4.
Predictive Profiling Protections
No citizen of the Grand Equestrian Republic may be subject to adverse civic, legal, or administrative action on the basis of predictive profiling — the use of data analytics, AI systems, or behavioral modeling to predict future conduct and act upon that prediction before any conduct has occurred. The constitutional right to be judged on one's actions, not on statistical predictions about one's future behavior, is fundamental to the rule of law and the presumption of innocence. Law enforcement may use predictive analysis as an investigative aid but may not take coercive action — arrest, restriction, surveillance, civic disqualification — on the basis of predictive output alone. Any citizen subject to a decision materially influenced by predictive profiling has the right to know, to challenge the profiling methodology before a Citizen Jury, and to have the decision reviewed by a human decision-maker.

Article XLI
⚖
Extended Economic Constitutionalism
Economic power left unaddressed by constitutional principle does not disappear — it migrates into political power through the paths of least resistance. This Article seals those paths.
Digital Finance & Resource Sovereignty
§ 1.
Digital Currency Governance
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes digital currencies — including central bank digital currencies, cryptocurrencies, and other digital financial instruments — as a domain requiring constitutional governance. The following constitutional principles apply: the Central Bank of the Republic has exclusive authority to issue any official digital currency of the Grand Equestrian Republic; no official digital currency may be designed or operated in a manner that allows the state to restrict, freeze, or reverse individual citizens' transactions without a lawful judicial warrant — the financial privacy of citizens is a constitutional right; private digital currencies may operate within the Republic subject to Fundamental Law regulation establishing transparency, consumer protection, and anti-money-laundering requirements; and no digital financial system — public or private — may be designed with surveillance capabilities that allow mass monitoring of citizens' financial activity without a Constitutional Jury authorization. Digital financial inclusion shall be a state obligation: no citizen shall be excluded from economic participation by inability to access digital financial systems.
§ 2.
Resource Sovereignty
The natural resources of the Grand Equestrian Republic — including its land, water, minerals, energy sources, forests, and marine resources — are the common heritage of its citizens across all generations. No natural resource may be alienated from the Republic's sovereignty by sale, lease, or treaty to any foreign entity in a manner that permanently extinguishes the Republic's ability to reclaim stewardship of that resource for the public good. The Assembly shall by Fundamental Law establish: the categories of natural resource that may not be fully privatized; the terms under which private entities may develop natural resources and the share of proceeds owed to the public trust; the environmental conditions that must be met before resource extraction is authorized; and the rights of territories and indigenous communities in whose lands resources are located. Resource sovereignty is held in trust for future generations; the present generation may use but may not permanently exhaust or alienate the natural inheritance of those who follow.
§ 3.
Corporate Constitutional Limits
Corporations, companies, and other legal entities operating within the jurisdiction of the Grand Equestrian Republic derive their legal existence from the laws of the Republic and are therefore constitutionally subordinate to the democratic order. The following constitutional limits apply to all corporate entities: no corporation may participate in, fund, direct, or materially influence any civic process of the Republic including lotteries, referenda, Assembly petitions, or constitutional conventions — corporate participation in civic processes constitutes an offence against the democratic order; corporations have no constitutional rights that override the rights of citizens — where corporate interests conflict with citizen rights, citizen rights prevail; corporations operating in sectors critical to the public interest may be subjected to public interest obligations by Fundamental Law without this constituting unlawful expropriation; and the state may not be captured by any corporate interest to the detriment of the public good — the revolving door between senior corporate positions and constitutional offices shall be governed by Fundamental Law establishing minimum cooling-off periods and disclosure requirements.
§ 4.
Speculative Concentration Limits
The Grand Equestrian Republic shall by Fundamental Law establish constitutional limits on speculative financial concentration — the accumulation of systemic risk in financial markets to a degree that creates leverage over the real economy and ultimately over democratic institutions. The constitutional principles governing speculative concentration are: no financial institution may become so large that its failure would require the state to prioritize its survival over the welfare of citizens — institutions of this size shall be subject to mandatory structural separation; speculative financial instruments that create systemic risk without corresponding economic productivity may be regulated, restricted, or prohibited by Fundamental Law; and the Central Bank shall maintain a Systemic Risk Register, published quarterly, identifying financial concentrations that pose risks to economic stability and democratic governance. The conversion of financial speculation into political power is a form of plutocracy prohibited by Article I §3(d).
§ 5.
AI-State Fusion Prevention
The Grand Equestrian Republic recognizes the long-horizon risk that governments may gradually delegate so many administrative, analytical, and decisional functions to artificial intelligence systems that democratic sovereignty becomes technically formal but operationally machine-mediated — a state that calls itself democratic but is effectively governed by algorithms optimizing for objectives set by a small technical class. The following constitutional safeguards prevent AI-State fusion: no AI system may be granted decision-making authority over matters affecting citizens' constitutional rights, civic standing, or access to public services without human review on demand by the affected citizen; the proportion of governmental functions delegated to AI systems shall be reported annually by the Council to the Assembly and reviewed against thresholds established by Fundamental Law; the Assembly shall retain exclusive human authority over all Fundamental Law; and no AI system's outputs shall be treated as binding without human certification of their constitutional compatibility. The technical sophistication of governance shall serve human judgment — it shall never replace it.

Article XLII
⚡
Constitutional Emergency Directorate & Civic Governance Academy
Speed beats deliberation during acute instability — but speed without accountability becomes tyranny. The Emergency Directorate is the Republic's answer to both dangers: operational clarity under crisis, with automatic expiration, judicial override, and post-crisis accountability built into its very structure.
Part A — The Constitutional Emergency Directorate
§ 1.
Establishment & Purpose
There is hereby established the Constitutional Emergency Directorate — a temporary, strictly time-limited executive coordination body that may be activated only during the most acute phases of existential national crisis, where the ordinary deliberative processes of the Assembly, the full Council, and the Rapid Action Council are demonstrably insufficient to coordinate a timely effective response. The Directorate does not replace the Assembly, the Council, or any constitutional institution. It operates alongside them with a single, narrow mandate: operational coordination during acute crisis. It holds no permanent authority, no legislative power, and no authority to make any decision of constitutional significance. It is a wartime operational instrument — temporary by design, constrained by structure, and automatically self-terminating.
§ 2.
Activation Conditions
The Constitutional Emergency Directorate may be activated only upon the simultaneous occurrence of all three of the following conditions: (a) A declared national emergency under Article XI that has been ratified by the Assembly; (b) A finding by the Rapid Action Council, by unanimous vote of all twenty-five members, that the pace of the emergency demonstrably exceeds the operational capacity of the Rapid Action Council itself to coordinate an effective response in real time; and (c) Confirmation by the Guardian Commission that the activation conditions are constitutionally satisfied and that no less extreme alternative is available. A Constitutional Jury of fifteen citizens, pre-selected by lot and held in permanent readiness, shall review the activation decision within twenty-four hours and may annul it by two-thirds vote.
§ 3.
Composition
Upon valid activation, the Constitutional Emergency Directorate shall consist of seven members: three selected by internal lot from the Rapid Action Council; two senior civil servants — the Crisis Coordinator under Article XXIV §4 and the senior General of the High Military Council — serving in professional capacity without political authority; one member of the Guardian Commission selected by internal lot; and one Legal Guide serving as Constitutional Advisor, whose sole function is to ensure every Directorate decision remains within constitutional bounds and to veto any proposed action that would exceed the Directorate's mandate, subject to immediate review by the standing Constitutional Jury.
§ 4.
Powers — Strictly Enumerated
The Constitutional Emergency Directorate shall have only the following powers, and no others: (a) issuing real-time operational coordination directives to all Departments, Agencies, and Defense Forces tiers regarding the specific emergency response; (b) activating pre-authorized emergency protocols established by the Assembly through Fundamental Law for the specific emergency type declared; (c) communicating directly with foreign governments on matters of immediate operational necessity; and (d) requisitioning public resources within the bounds of the existing Emergency Budget Reserve. The Directorate may not: pass or amend any law; suspend any constitutional right; alter the composition of any constitutional institution; direct the Guardian Commission; issue orders to the Courts; or extend its own activation.
§ 5.
Peacetime vs. Acute Crisis Mode & Automatic Expiration
The Constitutional Emergency Directorate expires automatically after fourteen days. It may be reactivated for a single additional fourteen-day period only by the same three-condition process of §2, with Assembly confirmation by absolute majority. After a total of twenty-eight days of Directorate activation in any single declared emergency, the Directorate may not be reactivated for that emergency under any circumstances. This hard limit defines the Grand Equestrian Republic's two governance modes: Peacetime Mode — full deliberative constitutional governance through all institutions; and Acute Crisis Mode — the Directorate's fourteen-day operational compression, strictly bounded and automatically self-terminating. There is no third mode. There is no permanent emergency. The Directorate is the constitutional firebreak between acute crisis and enduring tyranny.
§ 6.
Judicial Override & Post-Crisis Accountability
The standing Constitutional Jury of fifteen citizens shall sit continuously throughout any Directorate activation. Any citizen may petition this Jury to review any Directorate decision within six hours of its issuance; the Jury may suspend it by simple majority within twelve hours. All Directorate decisions shall be published in the Official Gazette within one hour of issuance. Within thirty days of expiration, the Public Auditor shall conduct a comprehensive Post-Crisis Accountability Audit of every decision, resource requisitioned, and directive issued. Any Directorate member found to have exceeded constitutional authority shall be immediately referred for impeachment.
Part B — The Civic Governance Academy
§ 7.
Establishment & Purpose
There is hereby established the Civic Governance Academy — a permanent constitutional institution responsible for providing all citizens selected by lot for civic office with the operational knowledge, constitutional literacy, and governance readiness required to serve effectively without compromising the democratic principle of sortition. The Academy addresses the central challenge of sortition-based governance: random selection protects against corruption, dynasties, and political capture, but randomness alone does not produce strategic thinking, technical literacy, or crisis judgment. The Academy ensures that every citizen who serves is genuinely prepared — not because they were selected for expertise, but because preparation was provided after they were selected at random.
§ 8.
Mandatory Induction Training
Every citizen selected by lot for any civic office carrying a term of thirty days or more shall, before assuming their duties, complete a mandatory Induction Training programme: free of charge; delivered in no more than thirty days for Assembly and Council members, proportionally shorter for other offices; available in all languages of the Republic; accessible remotely for geographically isolated citizens; and designed for a citizen with no prior governance experience. Training covers: the full Constitution in plain language; the specific powers, duties, and limits of the office to be held; the practical conduct of deliberation, voting, and procedure; the Civic Oath obligations; and plain-language introductions to key technical governance domains. Completion of Induction Training is a constitutional prerequisite for assuming qualifying civic office.
§ 9.
Expert Briefings & Crisis Simulations
Throughout their term, civic officers shall have access to a continuing Expert Briefing Programme: provided on demand for any technical subject before their body; delivered by domain experts appointed by the Judicial Rules Commission on the basis of knowledge and absence of conflict of interest; structured to present multiple perspectives and evidence ranges rather than single authoritative conclusions; and published in plain-language summary in the Official Gazette. The Academy shall also conduct mandatory annual Crisis Simulation exercises for all Assembly members, Council members, and Rapid Action Council members — presenting realistic scenarios of military threat, cyber attack, pandemic, economic collapse, and constitutional subversion. Simulation results shall not penalize officers but shall be published in aggregate to let citizens assess institutional governance readiness, and shall be used to continuously improve training content.
§ 10.
Academy Governance
The Civic Governance Academy shall be governed by a Board of seventeen citizens selected by lot, serving staggered three-year terms, with no Board member simultaneously holding any other civic office. The curriculum shall be reviewed every three years by an independent panel of twenty-one citizens by lot. It may not be directed by the Assembly, the Council, or any political or ideological body — it is based on constitutional text, evidence, and accumulated findings of Crisis Simulations and Dokimasia reviews. The Academy's budget is constitutionally guaranteed, audited annually by the Public Auditor, and its annual report on constitutional preparedness of the civic governance corps shall be published in the Official Gazette.
